## The Classies Eternal Dreamfall

### 4:00 a.m. — Before the world awakens

Adenta was still asleep.

The garden lay in the uncertain grey between night and morning. A few leaves trembled in the breeze, and somewhere beyond the wall, a dog barked at a disturbance that nobody else appeared to notice.

Inside the house, the bedroom clock changed to **04:00**.

The alarm sounded.

Enka reached across the bedside table, silenced it, and activated the day's Eternal Dreamfall reading. The narration began softly, guiding the room into the ancient world of the Septuagint—the Greek transmission of sacred writings that carried old words across languages, communities, and generations.

It was a fitting thought for someone whose work involved sending information across time.

Words could survive the fall of kingdoms. Wisdom could travel farther than its authors. But transmission alone did not guarantee understanding.

Enka listened for a while, then allowed the alarm's snooze function to take over.

At 05:00, the second alarm returned with less sympathy.

The YouVersion Verse of the Day appeared on the screen: Colossians 1:13. Rescue from darkness. A transfer from one dominion into another.

Enka sat up.

“Then the question,” he murmured, “is what we do after we're rescued.”

It was the sort of question that sounded simple until one became responsible for a team.

After his fitness session and morning preparations, he reviewed the day's priorities. The Millennium Timezone had returned two coordinates that continued to trouble the R&D department: a historical reference in Las Vegas and a probe date more than two centuries into the future.

Las Vegas was the reference.

Kuala Lumpur was the destination.

And somewhere between those two points lay an experiment that was beginning to attract more attention than the team had anticipated.

### 6:30 a.m. — The city begins to move

By half past six, Accra was shaking off the night.

Cars joined the morning traffic. Traders prepared their stalls. Commuters hurried towards transport connections, while bicycles threaded their way through quieter streets.

Enka's journey became an informal extension of Koware's work.

A conversation about a delayed delivery turned into a discussion about communication failures. A driver complained about people making promises before confirming that they could fulfil them. Enka listened, helped the two parties agree on a practical next step, and offered a Koware service card to the person who wanted more information.

The card carried a simple message: *Before you act, confirm what you know.*

It was not the most spectacular life hack in the world.

It was, however, the kind that prevented small misunderstandings from becoming expensive problems.

On the journey, a news update reported fresh calls for regular exercise and health screening following a community health walk in the Aburi area. Another report described public-health education and the distribution of HIV self-test kits at Pokuase Lorry Station in Greater Accra. ([gna.org.gh][5], [gna.org.gh][6])

Enka paused over the second report.

Health information was useful only when people could access it, understand it, and act on it without fear or stigma.

That thought stayed with him as the car approached Amrahia.

### 8:43 a.m. — The little house with the impossible laboratory

Koware Group's Amrahia headquarters looked much as it always did from the outside: a modest three-bedroom unit inside a gated community.

It did not look like the kind of place from which anyone would attempt to investigate the year 2222.

That was precisely why the neighbours watched it.

A resident peered through a window as another vehicle arrived. Someone else glanced towards the entrance, counted the people walking inside, and disappeared behind a curtain.

The unusual bursts of light from the R&D enclosure had become a neighbourhood topic.

One resident believed Koware was testing a new electrical system. Another suspected a private entertainment venture. A third had decided that the safest explanation was to assume the worst.

Inside, the team was less concerned with gossip than with telemetry.

Enka connected to the secure morning meeting. The Corporate Manager's briefing began with the week's objectives, current developments, and the latest information from Blue Crown R&D.

The Director of Research & Development, represented in the organisational chart by the IAPD Squad alias, had the mission update ready.

“The four-person team remains deployed,” the report began. “Communications are intermittent but functional. We have received additional environmental information from the probe zone.”

Enka looked at the laboratory specialist's latest status report.

“And the return conditions?”

“Not yet approved,” came the reply. “We still need to resolve an inconsistency in the equipment readings.”

Nobody spoke for a moment.

The experiment had been designed to gather useful information for Koware's products, services, and lifestyle packages. It had not been designed to prove that the equipment could operate indefinitely.

“Then we do not approve a return procedure based on incomplete readings,” Enka said. “And we do not continue simply because we have already invested time in the mission. Tell the team what we know, what we don't know, and what they must do if the link deteriorates.”

The instruction was recorded.

Outside, a neighbour saw a faint blue flash through the window.

Inside, the briefing moved to the historical reference.

### 9:12 a.m. — Three dates, one warning

The laboratory display opened into three columns.

The first carried the historical reference: **Las Vegas, 20 July 2003**.

The second showed the present: **Accra, 29 October 2026**.

The third displayed the probing destination: **Kuala Lumpur, 22 December 2222**.

The historical archive reminded the team of an old challenge familiar to desert cities: extreme heat, the demand for water, and the difficult balance between development and the resources required to sustain it. Contemporary Las Vegas reporting from July 2003 documented continuing debates over heat, water conservation, infrastructure, and growth. ([lasvegassun.com][7])

The current column showed a different kind of challenge. News from Kuala Lumpur reported that Malaysian customs had seized 30 server units containing advanced AI chips worth approximately RM49.5 million, or US$12.1 million, amid an investigation into suspected attempts to move restricted technology through the country. ([reuters.com][8])

The news was current reporting, not evidence of anything that would happen in 2222.

That distinction mattered.

“Technology can be valuable without being harmless,” Enka observed. “And information can be useful without being sufficient.”

The lab specialist nodded and opened the third column.

Its heading read:

**PROBE ARCHIVE — FICTIONAL FUTURE SCENARIO — 2222**

The system had no authentic newspaper archive from that date. What appeared beneath the heading was a scenario prepared for the mission, not verified future journalism.

The fictional report described a dispute over an automated urban rescue network. Its machines could identify people trapped in dangerous buildings, but a recent software change had left some emergency signals unconfirmed.

The headline was short:

*When the City Calls for Help, Who Answers?*

Nobody in the laboratory laughed.

The four-person team had been sent to investigate future developments that could inform Koware's products and services. Yet the fictional report raised a question that was relevant even in 2026: what happened when people trusted automated systems more than they trusted one another?

“Send them the safety advisory,” Enka instructed. “No assumptions about the report being real. Tell them to verify their environment, confirm every instruction, and stop if their equipment readings become unreliable.”

The lab specialist transmitted the message.

The acknowledgement indicator flashed once.

Then it disappeared.

### 10:06 a.m. — A signal from the other side

At the probing destination, the four-person team was examining an access point near an elevated transport structure.

The city around them was unfamiliar, yet recognisably Kuala Lumpur. The skyline retained traces of the architecture that had once defined the metropolis, while layers of later construction stretched between green corridors and elevated transport routes.

Their Blue Crown communications device vibrated.

“Message from Amrahia,” one of the team members announced.

The first few words arrived clearly. The rest dissolved into interference.

They tried again.

A fragment emerged:

“...confirm the environment... do not proceed... readings...”

Then silence.

One member wanted to continue towards the designated research site before the EduBeam charge diminished further.

Another insisted that the team stop and inspect its equipment.

Their disagreement lasted only seconds, but it exposed the central problem. The team could not safely distinguish between a delayed instruction, a corrupted instruction, and a new instruction that had never been fully received.

They stopped.

The team checked the device, compared its readings, confirmed each member's status, and attempted to re-establish communication.

No one was permitted to treat an incomplete message as authorisation to proceed.

Back in Amrahia, the specialist watched the telemetry return.

“Signal restored,” came the report.

Enka leaned forward.

“Ask them to confirm what they received.”

The response arrived moments later.

They had received enough of the message to understand the warning, but not enough to know the full reason behind it.

That was sufficient to justify stopping. It was not sufficient to justify resuming.

### 11:20 a.m. — The lesson behind the light

The Corporate Manager reopened the meeting.

The day's reading had started with ancient writings crossing the boundaries of language and time. It had moved through contemporary public-health reporting, a historical city confronting environmental pressures, and a fictional future in which automated rescue systems could not always be trusted.

Now the same principle had appeared inside Koware's own laboratory.

Rescue required more than reaching a destination.

It required knowing whether the person being helped could actually receive the help being offered.

It required systems that could admit uncertainty.

It required people willing to stop when the evidence was insufficient.

And it required someone to remain accountable when the equipment failed.

Enka returned to Colossians 1:13.

The verse spoke of rescue from darkness. In the mission briefing, darkness had become a practical metaphor for missing information, unreliable signals, and decisions made without sufficient knowledge.

The laboratory's safety lesson was therefore straightforward:

**When a safety-critical instruction is incomplete, stop, verify the situation, and obtain reliable confirmation before proceeding.**

No technological achievement was worth losing a team over an avoidable error.

The R&D specialist recorded the incident for further investigation. The team received revised instructions to maintain its safe position, conserve its remaining charge, and await a verified update.

The morning briefing continued, but the mood had changed.

The mission was still active.

Its next step would depend on evidence, not optimism.

### 12:03 p.m. — The neighbours have questions

Outside, the gated community had begun its ordinary midday rhythm.

A neighbour approached the entrance and asked why so many people were visiting the small house.

The liaison officer answered politely that the premises hosted research and administrative work, and that the team followed controlled operating procedures.

The explanation did not satisfy everyone.

One resident wanted to know about the flashes of light. Another complained that the unusual activity was unsettling. A third suggested that the residents should receive more information about anything that might affect their safety.

The liaison listened.

It would have been easy to dismiss the questions as interference. Instead, the officer recognised that the neighbours had their own legitimate interests.

Koware's confidentiality requirements mattered. So did responsible communication with the surrounding community.

The officer recorded the concerns for management review.

When the Corporate Manager received the report, he added another item to the day's action list: review how the laboratory's activities could be explained without disclosing protected research information.

The mission had generated a technical problem, a safety lesson, and a community-relations issue before lunchtime.

That was the reality of research.

The equipment occupied one room. Its consequences extended much farther.

### 4:40 p.m. — The record is updated

As the afternoon progressed, the team in Kuala Lumpur continued to report its status.

The communications link remained imperfect, but the members had established a safer procedure for handling incomplete messages. The laboratory specialist monitored the system and documented the anomaly rather than attempting an unverified correction.

Enka reviewed the day's findings.

The current news had reminded the team that advanced technology required accountability. The historical reference had demonstrated that communities could face persistent challenges even as their infrastructure developed. The fictional probe scenario had explored the danger of relying on automated rescue systems without reliable confirmation.

The team's own experience had brought those ideas together.

Enka entered a final note in the mission record:

*We must build systems that help people, but we must also build the procedures that protect them when the systems fail.*

He paused, then added a second line:

*The ability to send a signal is not the same as the ability to communicate.*

The specialist saved the entry.

The four personnel remained deployed, awaiting the next approved instruction.

Their return was not yet authorised.

Outside the laboratory, the evening light began to fade. A neighbour glanced towards the window once more, hoping to catch another glimpse of the mysterious blue glow.

Nothing happened.

For the moment, the house looked ordinary again.

But inside, the Millennium Timezone had begun recording a discrepancy that nobody could yet explain.

The displayed probe timestamp remained **22 December 2222, 09:36:58 UTC**.

The mission log showed the same destination.

The equipment log, however, contained a new entry: a return-channel signal had been registered at a time when the team had not transmitted one.

The specialist stared at the record.

“Enka,” they called quietly. “We have another signal.”

Enka returned to the screen.

“From Kuala Lumpur?”

The specialist checked the origin field.

“I can't confirm that.”

For several seconds, the room was silent.

Then the signal appeared again.

This time, the system captured three words before the connection vanished.

**“Do not return.”**

## Today's closing reflection

The day began with a question about what we do after rescue. It ends with a more difficult one: what should we do when the way home becomes uncertain?

Faith does not require recklessness. Courage does not require ignoring danger. And responsibility does not end when a system performs as expected.

Sometimes the wisest decision is to stop, examine the evidence, protect those in our care, and wait until the next step can be taken safely.

In Classies Chronicles, the light is not merely the flash of a teleportation device. It is also the knowledge that helps people recognise danger, the compassion that makes them help one another, and the wisdom to know when not to proceed.

**Tomorrow's question:** Who sent the warning—and why would anyone tell Koware's team not to return?

CLASSIES CHRONICLES — THE SIGNAL BEFORE SUNRISE

04:00 — ADENTA, GREATER ACCRA

The alarm sounded.

Outside, Adenta was still asleep. A few distant headlights moved along the road, while the occasional barking of a dog broke the silence.

Enka reached for his phone.

For a moment, he simply stared at the ceiling.

It had been an unusually demanding week.

The previous day's developments had brought the team closer to understanding the strange return-channel activity, but the more they examined the evidence, the less comfortable the situation became.

Four people remained deployed through the EduBeam.

Their destination was Kuala Lumpur, 22 December 2222.

They had intermittent communications, inconsistent equipment readings and an unexplained signal that nobody could authenticate.

Enka opened the Eternal Dreamfall reading plan.

The week's reading had progressed into the Septuagint, the ancient Greek translation of the Hebrew Scriptures.

He paused over the historical significance of the text.

Thousands of years separated the translators from the people who first recorded these writings. Yet the words had travelled through generations, languages and civilisations.

Preservation was not simply about keeping information.

It was about ensuring that the information remained meaningful and trustworthy.

He opened the day's recorded Verse of the Day.

Hosea 6:3:

"So let us know—let us press on to know the LORD. As surely as the sun rises, He will appear; He will come to us like the rain, like the spring showers that water the earth."

He read it again.

Pressing on to know.

Not merely believing that one knew, but continuing to seek understanding.

He thought about the four people in the future.

They needed answers. But they also needed to know when an answer was not yet available.

Sometimes, responsible leadership meant resisting the temptation to act.

06:30 — THE MORNING COMMUTE

By the time Enka began preparing for the day, the neighbourhood was becoming active.

The usual Accra morning movement had begun.

Vehicles joined the growing traffic, commercial drivers called out destinations, and pedestrians hurried towards their respective workplaces.

On the radio, the presenter discussed developments surrounding Ghana's energy sector, including the country's discussions with Russia's Rosatom concerning a proposed nuclear power station.

Enka listened.

Energy infrastructure, technological independence and national development were becoming increasingly important subjects.

He wondered how much of the country's future infrastructure would depend on systems that were still being researched today.

His thoughts returned to Blue Crown.

There was a considerable difference between developing technology that could perform an impressive demonstration and developing technology that could reliably protect the people using it.

The latter required discipline.

And discipline was exactly what the team needed.

09:00 — KOWARE GROUP, AMRAHIA

The morning briefing began.

Gifty Bright, Director of Research and Development, had already reviewed the previous night's laboratory records.

Obed Ali was at his workstation, examining the equipment logs.

Angie Sylvia was coordinating the communications records and maintaining the relevant liaison documentation.

Enka joined the meeting.

"Good morning, everyone."

There were the usual greetings before Gifty shared her screen.

"We have another development," she said.

The room became quiet.

The return-channel signal had appeared again.

This time, the recorded fragment was clearer.

Obed enlarged the available information.

"Before we discuss its meaning, I want to establish something," he said. "We have captured a message. We have not authenticated its source."

Enka nodded.

"Continue."

The message appeared on the screen.

DO NOT RETURN.

Nobody spoke immediately.

Angie looked at the message again.

"Is that all we have?"

"That's all we can establish from the captured transmission," Obed replied.

Gifty leaned forward.

"We cannot yet determine whether this is a genuine warning, corrupted system output, an external transmission or a temporal anomaly."

Enka looked around the room.

"Then we will not pretend that we know."

He asked Gifty to preserve the original laboratory records.

Obed was instructed to maintain an independent technical record.

Angie would document the communication attempts and any response received from the deployed personnel.

No one was authorised to modify the original evidence.

The message would remain exactly as captured.

10:15 — THE FOUR-PERSON TEAM

Far away from the laboratory, in the fictional future environment of Kuala Lumpur, the four deployed personnel continued their assignment.

They had been instructed to conserve their remaining resources, maintain their observation records and avoid unnecessary movements.

The team had become accustomed to intermittent communication.

Sometimes a message arrived almost immediately.

At other times, the connection disappeared before a complete exchange could take place.

Their Blue Crown communication devices remained their principal means of contacting the R&D team.

When the latest instruction arrived, they read it carefully.

The message from Amrahia was straightforward:

Maintain position. Preserve energy. Do not attempt an unauthorised return. Continue recording observations.

One of the team members looked towards the others.

"So we wait?"

"For now," another replied.

Nobody was particularly pleased with the situation.

They had originally travelled to gather information that could support Koware's products, services and lifestyle research.

They had not expected an assignment in which the most important discovery might be something they were unable to explain.

Nevertheless, they continued their work.

There were still observations to record.

There were still things to learn.

And there was a responsibility to return safely.

12:30 — THE COST OF AN ANSWER

Back at Amrahia, Enka reviewed the developing situation.

The message had created another problem.

If it was a warning, ignoring it could expose the team to danger.

If it was corrupted information, following it could be equally dangerous.

And if it originated from an external source, the team needed to establish how that source had gained access to the return channel.

Gifty suggested comparing the transmission with the historical Singapore event of 9 July 2026.

The proposal was accepted, but with an important qualification.

A resemblance between two signals did not establish that they had the same origin.

The records would be examined independently.

Enka requested a formal decision document containing:

The verified mission status.
Available communication records.
Equipment inconsistencies.
Environmental observations.
Remaining operational resources.
The exact wording of the warning.
Possible explanations.
Unresolved technical questions.
The authority responsible for approving any return.

He was particularly concerned about the four people.

The organisation had a responsibility to protect its personnel.

A successful research mission was not one that simply brought back valuable information.

It was one that achieved its objectives without unnecessarily sacrificing the people entrusted with the assignment.

15:00 — THE NEIGHBOURS

Outside, the Amrahia neighbourhood continued its usual afternoon activities.

Some neighbours had become accustomed to the unusual movements around the Koware premises.

Others remained curious.

The occasional unusual burst of light associated with previous EduBeam activities had not escaped everyone's attention.

A neighbour passing the property noticed activity around the building.

"These people are always doing something interesting," he remarked.

Another laughed.

"Perhaps they are developing something big."

Neither knew how close the organisation was to a difficult decision.

Inside, Obed was still working through the records.

He had separated the original signal capture from the subsequent laboratory interpretations.

The distinction mattered.

A record was evidence of what had been captured.

An interpretation was an explanation of what someone believed it meant.

Those were not the same thing.

He added another entry to the technical record.

SOURCE: UNAUTHENTICATED.

MESSAGE: DO NOT RETURN.

STATUS: UNDER INVESTIGATION.

17:30 — THE DECISION

Enka reconvened the team.

The question was no longer whether the message was interesting.

It was whether anyone could demonstrate that returning was safe.

Nobody could.

The mission's original objectives remained valuable, but the available evidence did not justify proceeding as though everything was normal.

Enka made his position clear.

"We are not going to gamble with four people's lives because we are impatient to complete an assignment."

He instructed the team to maintain the suspension.

No improvised rescue.

No unauthorised return.

No alteration of the original records.

The R&D department would continue investigating the transmission while the deployed personnel maintained their safety and communication procedures.

Gifty agreed.

Obed confirmed the documentation requirements.

Angie recorded the decision.

For the moment, the organisation had chosen caution over convenience.

21:00 — ADENTA

The day was drawing to a close.

Enka had returned home.

He sat quietly, reflecting on the events of the day.

He thought about the historical reference in Las Vegas in 2003, the present operations in Accra and the four people occupying a very different temporal environment.

Three different periods.

One organisation.

One responsibility.

The day's Bible reading returned to his mind.

To press on to know the Lord.

There was something important about accepting that knowledge takes time.

Not every unanswered question needs an immediate answer.

Not every opportunity requires immediate action.

And not every warning should be ignored simply because its origin is unknown.

His phone vibrated.

A notification from the R&D team appeared.

The laboratory had received another update.

He opened it.

There was no new explanation.

No authenticated sender.

No confirmed technical diagnosis.

Only the original message, preserved in the system.

DO NOT RETURN.

Enka placed the phone on the table.

Tomorrow would be Saturday.

There would be time to reflect, review the week's developments and consider the next steps.

The four-person team remained deployed.

The investigation remained open.

And the most important question had changed.

It was no longer simply:

Who sent the message?

It was now:

What happens if the message is right?

END OF DAY 10.

CLASSIES CHRONICLES
The Signal Before Sunrise
Part 10 — The Message That Refused to Explain Itself

Friday, 30 October 2026
Episode: Day 10 of 10
Chapter: October–December Term
Present-day locations: Adenta → Amrahia, Greater Accra, Ghana
Historical reference: Las Vegas, 20 July 2003
EduBeam destination: Kuala Lumpur, 22 December 2222
Mission status: Active — Deployment Suspended Pending Verification

THE MESSAGE THAT REFUSED TO EXPLAIN ITSELF
Friday, 30 October 2026
CLASSIES CHRONICLES — THE SIGNAL BEFORE SUNRISE

04:00 — ADENTA, GREATER ACCRA

The alarm sounded.

Outside, Adenta was still asleep. A few distant headlights moved along the road, while the occasional barking of a dog broke the silence.

Enka reached for his phone.

For a moment, he simply stared at the ceiling.

It had been an unusually demanding week.

The previous day's developments had brought the team closer to understanding the strange return-channel activity, but the more they examined the evidence, the less comfortable the situation became.

Four people remained deployed through the EduBeam.

Their destination was Kuala Lumpur, 22 December 2222.

They had intermittent communications, inconsistent equipment readings and an unexplained signal that nobody could authenticate.

Enka opened the Eternal Dreamfall reading plan.

The week's reading had progressed into the Septuagint, the ancient Greek translation of the Hebrew Scriptures.

He paused over the historical significance of the text.

Thousands of years separated the translators from the people who first recorded these writings. Yet the words had travelled through generations, languages and civilisations.

Preservation was not simply about keeping information.

It was about ensuring that the information remained meaningful and trustworthy.

He opened the day's recorded Verse of the Day.

Hosea 6:3:

"So let us know—let us press on to know the LORD. As surely as the sun rises, He will appear; He will come to us like the rain, like the spring showers that water the earth."

He read it again.

Pressing on to know.

Not merely believing that one knew, but continuing to seek understanding.

He thought about the four people in the future.

They needed answers. But they also needed to know when an answer was not yet available.

Sometimes, responsible leadership meant resisting the temptation to act.

06:30 — THE MORNING COMMUTE

By the time Enka began preparing for the day, the neighbourhood was becoming active.

The usual Accra morning movement had begun.

Vehicles joined the growing traffic, commercial drivers called out destinations, and pedestrians hurried towards their respective workplaces.

On the radio, the presenter discussed developments surrounding Ghana's energy sector, including the country's discussions with Russia's Rosatom concerning a proposed nuclear power station.

Enka listened.

Energy infrastructure, technological independence and national development were becoming increasingly important subjects.

He wondered how much of the country's future infrastructure would depend on systems that were still being researched today.

His thoughts returned to Blue Crown.

There was a considerable difference between developing technology that could perform an impressive demonstration and developing technology that could reliably protect the people using it.

The latter required discipline.

And discipline was exactly what the team needed.

09:00 — KOWARE GROUP, AMRAHIA

The morning briefing began.

Gifty Bright, Director of Research and Development, had already reviewed the previous night's laboratory records.

Obed Ali was at his workstation, examining the equipment logs.

Angie Sylvia was coordinating the communications records and maintaining the relevant liaison documentation.

Enka joined the meeting.

"Good morning, everyone."

There were the usual greetings before Gifty shared her screen.

"We have another development," she said.

The room became quiet.

The return-channel signal had appeared again.

This time, the recorded fragment was clearer.

Obed enlarged the available information.

"Before we discuss its meaning, I want to establish something," he said. "We have captured a message. We have not authenticated its source."

Enka nodded.

"Continue."

The message appeared on the screen.

DO NOT RETURN.

Nobody spoke immediately.

Angie looked at the message again.

"Is that all we have?"

"That's all we can establish from the captured transmission," Obed replied.

Gifty leaned forward.

"We cannot yet determine whether this is a genuine warning, corrupted system output, an external transmission or a temporal anomaly."

Enka looked around the room.

"Then we will not pretend that we know."

He asked Gifty to preserve the original laboratory records.

Obed was instructed to maintain an independent technical record.

Angie would document the communication attempts and any response received from the deployed personnel.

No one was authorised to modify the original evidence.

The message would remain exactly as captured.

10:15 — THE FOUR-PERSON TEAM

Far away from the laboratory, in the fictional future environment of Kuala Lumpur, the four deployed personnel continued their assignment.

They had been instructed to conserve their remaining resources, maintain their observation records and avoid unnecessary movements.

The team had become accustomed to intermittent communication.

Sometimes a message arrived almost immediately.

At other times, the connection disappeared before a complete exchange could take place.

Their Blue Crown communication devices remained their principal means of contacting the R&D team.

When the latest instruction arrived, they read it carefully.

The message from Amrahia was straightforward:

Maintain position. Preserve energy. Do not attempt an unauthorised return. Continue recording observations.

One of the team members looked towards the others.

"So we wait?"

"For now," another replied.

Nobody was particularly pleased with the situation.

They had originally travelled to gather information that could support Koware's products, services and lifestyle research.

They had not expected an assignment in which the most important discovery might be something they were unable to explain.

Nevertheless, they continued their work.

There were still observations to record.

There were still things to learn.

And there was a responsibility to return safely.

12:30 — THE COST OF AN ANSWER

Back at Amrahia, Enka reviewed the developing situation.

The message had created another problem.

If it was a warning, ignoring it could expose the team to danger.

If it was corrupted information, following it could be equally dangerous.

And if it originated from an external source, the team needed to establish how that source had gained access to the return channel.

Gifty suggested comparing the transmission with the historical Singapore event of 9 July 2026.

The proposal was accepted, but with an important qualification.

A resemblance between two signals did not establish that they had the same origin.

The records would be examined independently.

Enka requested a formal decision document containing:

The verified mission status.
Available communication records.
Equipment inconsistencies.
Environmental observations.
Remaining operational resources.
The exact wording of the warning.
Possible explanations.
Unresolved technical questions.
The authority responsible for approving any return.

He was particularly concerned about the four people.

The organisation had a responsibility to protect its personnel.

A successful research mission was not one that simply brought back valuable information.

It was one that achieved its objectives without unnecessarily sacrificing the people entrusted with the assignment.

15:00 — THE NEIGHBOURS

Outside, the Amrahia neighbourhood continued its usual afternoon activities.

Some neighbours had become accustomed to the unusual movements around the Koware premises.

Others remained curious.

The occasional unusual burst of light associated with previous EduBeam activities had not escaped everyone's attention.

A neighbour passing the property noticed activity around the building.

"These people are always doing something interesting," he remarked.

Another laughed.

"Perhaps they are developing something big."

Neither knew how close the organisation was to a difficult decision.

Inside, Obed was still working through the records.

He had separated the original signal capture from the subsequent laboratory interpretations.

The distinction mattered.

A record was evidence of what had been captured.

An interpretation was an explanation of what someone believed it meant.

Those were not the same thing.

He added another entry to the technical record.

SOURCE: UNAUTHENTICATED.

MESSAGE: DO NOT RETURN.

STATUS: UNDER INVESTIGATION.

17:30 — THE DECISION

Enka reconvened the team.

The question was no longer whether the message was interesting.

It was whether anyone could demonstrate that returning was safe.

Nobody could.

The mission's original objectives remained valuable, but the available evidence did not justify proceeding as though everything was normal.

Enka made his position clear.

"We are not going to gamble with four people's lives because we are impatient to complete an assignment."

He instructed the team to maintain the suspension.

No improvised rescue.

No unauthorised return.

No alteration of the original records.

The R&D department would continue investigating the transmission while the deployed personnel maintained their safety and communication procedures.

Gifty agreed.

Obed confirmed the documentation requirements.

Angie recorded the decision.

For the moment, the organisation had chosen caution over convenience.

21:00 — ADENTA

The day was drawing to a close.

Enka had returned home.

He sat quietly, reflecting on the events of the day.

He thought about the historical reference in Las Vegas in 2003, the present operations in Accra and the four people occupying a very different temporal environment.

Three different periods.

One organisation.

One responsibility.

The day's Bible reading returned to his mind.

To press on to know the Lord.

There was something important about accepting that knowledge takes time.

Not every unanswered question needs an immediate answer.

Not every opportunity requires immediate action.

And not every warning should be ignored simply because its origin is unknown.

His phone vibrated.

A notification from the R&D team appeared.

The laboratory had received another update.

He opened it.

There was no new explanation.

No authenticated sender.

No confirmed technical diagnosis.

Only the original message, preserved in the system.

DO NOT RETURN.

Enka placed the phone on the table.

Tomorrow would be Saturday.

There would be time to reflect, review the week's developments and consider the next steps.

The four-person team remained deployed.

The investigation remained open.

And the most important question had changed.

It was no longer simply:

Who sent the message?

It was now:

What happens if the message is right?

END OF DAY 10.
