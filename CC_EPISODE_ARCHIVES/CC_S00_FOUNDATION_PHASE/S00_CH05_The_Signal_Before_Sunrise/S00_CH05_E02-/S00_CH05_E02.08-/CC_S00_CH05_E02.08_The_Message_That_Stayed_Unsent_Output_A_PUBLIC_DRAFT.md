### Wednesday, 11 November — The empty space

The first thing the Legal Journalist removed from the reception pinboard was not a headline. It was a word.

**CONFIRMED.**

The black marker had gone straight through it. Underneath, the word that replaced it was smaller: **REPORTED**.

In Koware's imagined headquarters at New Legon, Accra, the board had become a kind of town square. Staff paused before it on their way to their desks. A visitor might read an announcement about a proposed business fair; someone preparing a project could see a warning about a changing service; a person who had spent too long on the road might stop beside a notice about transport and ask whether it was a promise or just another rumour.

The Legal Journalist wanted the board to make that difference visible. Each new card had room for a source, publication date, verification status and a note about why Koware might care. The digital pinboard had the same fields, although it was considerably easier to make the digital one look authoritative.

The Public Relations & Press Coordinator studied the latest draft. “The description says the Kuala Lumpur instructions were *approved and distributed*.”

“Distributed is what the work order suggests,” replied the Marketing Manager. “Approved is what we keep trying to prove.”

They were discussing an unresolved *fictional* field investigation carried over from the previous draft installment. At a Kuala Lumpur facility, four versions of an emergency notice appeared in the working record. Three pointed in one direction, and a fourth in another. A distribution reference, **Work Order 44-E**, proved no more than a trace of circulation. A sealed archive box might contain an earlier instruction, but no properly authorized release had yet been established. And even an authentic old instruction could be dangerous if the building had since changed.

On a separate screen, an unfinished note from Enka asked the one question nobody could polish away: *Who has the right to tell people which version to use?*

The Public Relations desk declined to publish the team's conclusion as an official safety notice. The Marketing desk withdrew a dramatic headline it had drafted for an imagined campaign. They agreed to keep the disagreement visible inside the editorial workroom without presenting any disputed route as safe.

Enka looked at the day's Bible App reference, **Matthew 6:11**, and considered the modest demands contained in a request for daily provision. Sometimes what a person needed next was not the entire answer; it was reliable information enough to take one responsible step. In the Eternal Dreamfall notebook, he wrote: *A useful document must tell you not only where to go, but what you may trust about the instruction.*

Across the room, the Legal Journalist reserved a blank rectangle on the pinboard: **AUTHORITY NOT YET VERIFIED**.

The blank space was an honest piece of reporting.

### Thursday, 12 November — The cost of a wrong verb

By Thursday the International Relations desk was working from three separate questions. What was the instruction's original source? Who could authorize it? And who would be accountable for checking that the instruction was suitable for the building as it existed now?

The Global Partnerships Registrar arranged copies of the fictional Kuala Lumpur paperwork by document number instead of by the order in which they had arrived. The Global Partnerships Liaison set a second register beside the first: one column for the language of each version, another for its recipient, and a third for whether delivery had actually been acknowledged.

“An acknowledgement is not comprehension,” the liaison said. “And comprehension is not permission.”

Nobody on the desk claimed to know the deployed team's individual whereabouts or condition. The team's supplied geographic position remained Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia; no fresh authenticated field communication was available to the office. The staff were examining the copies already in the story's draft record, not pretending the team had sent a new report.

On Enka's research display were three clocks, each with a different purpose. The **present** belonged to the work being drafted in Accra in November 2026. The **historical-reference** setting pointed to **22 July 2003, Las Vegas**, a place whose old paper plans Enka used as an *imagined* Dreamfall analogy—not as a newly discovered newspaper report or proof that he had been there. The **Millennium Probe** setting projected a fictional Kuala Lumpur coordinate on **26 April 2108**. None of those clocks established a team member's location or an emergency route's validity. A possible future was not a message from the field.

The distinction troubled Enka more than the science-fiction machinery. Technology could move dates across a display in a second; people still had to determine what those dates meant.

Later in the day, the Legal Journalist queued an item on Ghana's 2027 Budget for verification before posting it. When the finance minister presented the budget on Thursday, the reported **US$10 billion New Economy programme over four years**, with a **GH¢30 billion initial allocation for 2027**, gave the Marketing desk something concrete to analyze. It was not a Koware contract, grant approval or authority to announce a partnership.

The International Relations desk watched a different developing story: disruption to widely used Microsoft communications services. That report posed a practical question. If an important approval request disappeared inside an inaccessible inbox, how would anyone demonstrate what had actually been sent, received or authorized? The team proposed a secondary contact channel and an offline record of decisions. They did not claim that Koware itself had suffered an outage.

That evening the registrar wrote a careful annotation beside the sealed-box reference: *Location indicated; contents unknown; release authority unresolved.* The line did not solve the mystery. It prevented the mystery from being mistaken for a solution.

Enka returned to **James 4:17**, Thursday's Bible App reference, and thought about the responsibility of knowing a necessary action but failing to do it. He saw no virtue in acting on a dangerous guess. The right action might be to preserve evidence, seek the competent local authority and refuse to turn an incomplete record into an instruction.

### Friday, 13 November — The message that stayed unsent

On Friday morning, the legal newsroom and reception pinboard were more crowded than at any other point that week. The verified headlines concerned the New Economy budget, a Ghana Stock Exchange discussion of blockchain and capital-market modernization, and the opening of the **Ecobank JoyBusiness SME Fair at GHUD Park, Accra Mall**, scheduled for **13–15 November**. A separate international card described Malaysia's investigation into possible AI-chip re-exports linked to Speedmatrix, a local unit of Megaspeed. The journalists had underlined *investigation*: it was not an established finding that the chips had been re-exported or that a company had broken the law.

The headlines mattered to different people for different reasons.

The Events & Tours Coordinator saw the fair as an opportunity to observe Ghanaian businesses and perhaps prepare a later visit. The Community Outreach Officer wanted to know whether there would be a legitimate, accessible way to learn what smaller exhibitors actually needed. The Booking Agent had a spreadsheet of interested staff and a separate space for confirmed arrangements.

“Those names mean they would *like* to attend,” the Booking Agent said. “We don't have a confirmed booking.”

“Then let's not put anyone on a bus in our meeting minutes,” the coordinator replied.

The proposed visit stayed proposed. Elsewhere the International Relations team separated the Malaysia chip investigation from Koware's fictional Kuala Lumpur document inquiry. They took place in the same country; they did not establish the same facts. The Legal Journalist kept both subjects on the pinboard with independent source references.

Before the morning briefing, the Booking Agent tried to slide an overloaded folder under the meeting-room door while balancing a stack of loose attendance forms. The folder wedged, the forms sprang upward, and a large sheet landed squarely on the agent's head like an oversized paper hat. For one startled second the paper proclaimed **CONFIRMED ATTENDANCE** over a face that could see nothing. The coordinator lifted it off and laid it on the table, where it belonged. The single ridiculous accident did more to expose their labelling problem than an hour of complaints could have done.

At the meeting, Edliex—Koware's AI, pronounced *Alex*—remained in listening mode while the desks reported. Public Relations had corrected the language of an unsupported claim. Marketing had opened a budget-opportunity research note without promising a contract. International Relations and Partnerships had preserved an unresolved question of authority, version and translation. Events, Outreach and Booking had separated interest from confirmed attendance.

Only after the reports did the Corporate Manager invite Edliex to give feedback. Its listening indicator changed to feedback mode.

“Each desk encountered a document that could travel farther than its authority,” Edliex said. “A forwarded report is not a regulatory finding. A budget announcement is not an award to Koware. An event enquiry is not a reservation. A work order for a notice is not approval of the notice.”

The indicator changed again: conversational mode.

“Then what can we actually do?” the manager asked.

“We can build a trace,” Edliex replied. “Not a new emergency instruction. A trace that asks where a source came from, who is allowed to approve it, which revision applies, whether it still matches the place concerned, whether translations preserve its meaning, and whether the intended people received and understood it.”

The room was quiet. Outside, Accra traffic continued with no interest in their unfinished paperwork.

“The responsible local safety authority must determine current safe guidance,” Edliex added. “Koware's fictional inquiry does not make this office the approving authority for an unfamiliar building.”

Someone suggested sending the Kuala Lumpur team a confident message before close of business: *Proceed using the three matching notices until we find the fourth one's source.* The Corporate Manager left the sentence on the screen long enough for everyone to read it.

Then the manager deleted it.

Instead the group prepared a *request for verification*, not an operational command: preserve the four copies, ask the proper custodian about the sealed box through an authorized channel, seek qualified current local guidance, and establish who can confirm the approved and applicable notice. Because the field channel was not authenticated in the available record, the draft was placed in an **unsent** queue for editorial and authority review. Nothing in the room proved the deployed team had received it.

Enka remembered the Friday Bible App reference, **Psalm 139:10**, with its image of guidance even at a great distance. He did not treat the verse as proof of a technology or a safety decision. It reminded him that care for someone far away could mean being precise about what one did **not** know.

On the Dreamfall display, the old Las Vegas clock and the imagined future Kuala Lumpur clock sat on either side of Accra's present. The Edubeam concept could make three centuries appear adjacent. It could not replace the missing permission line in a document drawn up by people.

At closing, the Legal Journalist left the week's significant headlines on the reception board for continued investigation. Under them was a modest new card: **UNVERIFIED IS NOT UNSAFE TO DISCUSS. IT IS UNSAFE TO PRESENT AS AN AUTHORIZED INSTRUCTION.** The office had not opened the sealed archive box, certified a route, confirmed a booking, dispatched a new field order or resolved the mystery. It had done one less spectacular thing: it had prevented an uncertain instruction from acquiring false authority simply by being copied again.

The draft message remained in the unsent queue. In the room's final light, the empty authorization field was no longer a flaw someone wanted to hide. It was the boundary they had decided to respect.

A team has several copies of an urgent notice, a record showing that they were distributed, and a possible archive reference. The copies disagree on a safety-critical direction. **Which response best respects the evidence and the people who may rely on it?**

**A.** Use the direction repeated on the greatest number of copies and investigate the others later.  
**B.** Treat the distribution record as proof that the sender approved every version.  
**C.** Ask staff to test both routes without local professional guidance.  
**D.** Publish the newest-looking copy immediately so everyone has one instruction.  
**E.** Preserve the conflicting versions, establish the competent approving authority and current local guidance, and communicate only what has been verified.
