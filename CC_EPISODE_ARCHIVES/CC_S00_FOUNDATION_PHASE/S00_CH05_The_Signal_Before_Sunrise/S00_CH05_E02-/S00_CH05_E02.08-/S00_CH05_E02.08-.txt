## **THE VERSION THAT REACHED EVERYONE**

*Friday, 13 November 2026 — New Legon, Accra*

### **1. The board before the meeting**

By the time the first employees arrived at Koware headquarters, the reception pinboard had acquired another layer of paper.

The Legal Journalist had been busy.

Three headlines occupied the upper section.

**GHANA'S 2027 BUDGET: A NEW ECONOMY AND A GH¢30 BILLION INVESTMENT PLAN.**

**ECOBANK JOYBUSINESS SME FAIR 2026 OPENS AT ACCRA MALL.**

**MICROSOFT STRUGGLES TO RESTORE OUTLOOK AND TEAMS AFTER A GLOBAL SERVICE DISRUPTION.**

A fourth notice had been placed beneath them.

**TELECEL CASH INTRODUCES AN E-WALLET AND ONLINE SHOPPING PLATFORM.**

The headlines represented four different developments, but one question connected them.

How prepared was Koware to operate when the systems, information and arrangements people depended on changed?

The Events and Tours Coordinator stopped in front of the board.

The SME Fair caught her attention.

It was an opportunity to observe Ghanaian businesses, discover emerging service providers and assess potential partnerships.

She took a photograph of the headline and added it to her morning work notes.

The Community Outreach Officer joined her.

"Are we attending?"

"That is what I am trying to establish."

"Then we need to know whether we are visitors, exhibitors or simply observers."

The Booking Agent arrived with a small folder.

"I have something that might help."

He placed a printed document on the desk.

It contained a venue reference, an event date and a provisional arrangement.

The Events and Tours Coordinator examined it.

"Is this confirmed?"

"Well, the venue has acknowledged the request."

"That wasn't my question."

The Booking Agent looked again.

The document said *request received*.

Not *booking confirmed*.

He nodded.

"Right. We have an acknowledgement, not a reservation."

It was a small distinction, but it was the difference between planning an event and pretending one had already been arranged.

### **2. The notice from Kuala Lumpur**

At approximately the same time, a new message appeared on the internal communications screen.

It concerned the continuing Kuala Lumpur investigation.

The deployed team had been examining an emergency notice distributed in four languages.

The original investigation remained unresolved.

Nobody had yet established conclusively:

- Who originally approved the instruction.
- Which authority was responsible for issuing it.
- When the document was revised.
- Which version was effective.
- Whether all four translations preserved the same meaning.
- Who was responsible for confirming the authoritative version.

The latest message contained a short observation.

One of the copies circulating among the relevant parties appeared to have a different revision reference from another copy.

That did not establish which document was correct.

It established that the discrepancy needed investigation.

The Events and Tours Coordinator read the message twice.

"If we are organizing an international visit, we cannot assume that everyone has received the same instructions simply because everyone has received a document."

The Community Outreach Officer agreed.

"And a translation can be grammatically correct but still fail to communicate the intended instruction."

The Booking Agent opened his folder again.

"Then we should start with the document register. Original, revision, approval, distribution."

The three looked at one another.

Their respective responsibilities were different, but the problem had exposed a common weakness.

A document could travel faster than its authority.

### **3. Edliex enters the conversation**

The morning meeting began shortly afterwards.

The Corporate Manager took his place at the table.

The Legal Journalist's headlines were brought into the discussion, and the three employees presented their observations.

Edliex, Koware's AI, was already listening.

The AI remained in observation mode during the initial discussion.

The first subject was the SME Fair.

The Events and Tours Coordinator explained the potential value of attending, but distinguished between a confirmed booking and an acknowledged request.

The Community Outreach Officer proposed identifying relevant exhibitors and preparing a list of potential contacts.

The Booking Agent recommended that no travel or venue commitment should be represented as confirmed until written confirmation was obtained.

The second subject was the Kuala Lumpur emergency notice.

The Corporate Manager listened.

"Can we establish which of the four versions is authoritative?"

The response was cautious.

"Not yet."

"Can we establish whether the translations are equivalent?"

"Not without the original approved document and qualified comparison."

The Corporate Manager looked towards the communications screen.

"Then we have two separate problems. One concerns the authority of the instruction. The other concerns the accuracy of its distribution."

Edliex was activated.

Its status indicator changed from passive listening to conversational mode.

**EDLIEX:**

"May I offer an observation?"

"Proceed."

"Both discussions involve the difference between an acknowledgement and an authorization.

"The venue has acknowledged a request. That does not establish a confirmed booking.

"The Kuala Lumpur recipients have received documents. That does not establish that they received the currently authorized instruction.

"In both cases, the administrative evidence must be distinguished from the actual decision."

The Corporate Manager nodded.

"Continue."

"Three verification questions may help:

"First, what is the original source?

"Second, who has authority to approve the relevant version?

"Third, what evidence establishes that the intended recipient received the correct version?"

The Community Outreach Officer leaned forward.

"That third question matters particularly when the recipients speak different languages."

"Correct," Edliex replied. "Distribution is not the same as comprehension."

There was a brief silence.

Then the Booking Agent spoke.

"So, effectively, we should stop celebrating when the email arrives."

Edliex responded:

"That would be a reasonable beginning."

### **4. The lesson behind the paperwork**

The meeting moved to the wider implications.

The Microsoft disruption had highlighted the vulnerability of relying on a single communication platform.

Koware did not have evidence that its own services had been affected, but the headline was relevant to its business continuity planning.

The Corporate Manager asked what could be improved.

The Booking Agent suggested maintaining accessible records of confirmed arrangements outside a single email thread.

The Events and Tours Coordinator recommended a version-controlled itinerary and a clearly identified contact responsible for updates.

The Community Outreach Officer proposed ensuring that important instructions were communicated in a form recipients could understand and use.

Edliex summarized the emerging principle.

"An effective communication system needs more than successful transmission.

"It needs authority, version control, understandable content, recipient verification and a reliable fallback."

The Corporate Manager considered the proposal.

"That should become part of our working approach."

He then turned towards the Kuala Lumpur message.

"But we must not confuse a proposed improvement with a completed investigation."

Nobody objected.

The original emergency notice still needed to be obtained.

Its approval history still needed to be established.

The four translations still needed to be compared against the authoritative source.

And the person or authority responsible for resolving the discrepancy still needed to be identified.

The team had a clearer investigation plan.

It did not yet have an answer.

### **5. The reception pinboard incident**

After the meeting, the Events and Tours Coordinator returned to the reception area to reorganize the headlines.

The SME Fair notice was moved beside the 2027 Budget headline.

The Microsoft disruption was placed under a new heading:

**BUSINESS CONTINUITY AND COMMUNICATIONS.**

The Community Outreach Officer handed her another sheet.

The Booking Agent reached for the drawing pins.

At that exact moment, the entire bottom row of the pinboard detached.

Four sheets, two photographs and a collection of drawing pins descended onto the desk.

For several seconds, everyone stared at the mess.

The Booking Agent looked down at his folder.

"At least these documents have now been successfully distributed."

Nobody spoke.

Then the Community Outreach Officer laughed.

Even Edliex registered a short pause before responding.

"Distribution confirmed. Display integrity failed."

It was the only interruption the morning needed.

"Then we keep the distinction."

He closed the file.

The Kuala Lumpur investigation was still open.

The four-language notice remained unresolved.

The SME Fair arrangement was still awaiting confirmation.

And the reception pinboard needed repairing.

## The Name Beside the Journey

**Friday, 13 November 2026**  
*Provisional Foundation-phase daily installment · Working reference `S00_CH05_E02.10` · Fictional editorial draft; not approved for public release*

At Koware’s New Legon office, the guest list had two versions of the same name.

Neither version was obviously wrong. One matched a booking note; the other matched a community message. A third document gave a headcount but no names at all.

The Events & Tours Coordinator laid the pages side by side. “We can plan around the number,” she said. “But we can’t pretend the number tells us who needs what.”

The Community Outreach Officer had been listening. “And we shouldn’t ask people to explain private details just to make our list look complete.”

The Booking Agent turned the reservation sheet around so everyone could see its blank fields. “Then this is a draft list, not a confirmed guest list. I can hold the place in the plan without marking anybody as booked.”

She reached for a roll of removable labels. It unspooled farther than expected, crossed the table, and attached itself neatly to the Coordinator’s sleeve.

For a brief moment, the Coordinator stood wearing a white label that read **PENDING**.

The Booking Agent freed it. The Coordinator inspected the label, then placed it beside the papers instead of throwing it away.

“Useful status,” she said. “Wrong location.”

The room laughed once, and the work resumed.

At the morning meeting, Edliex had listened while the three roles described what was known, what was missing, and what they were not authorized to promise. When invited to respond, the AI displayed a short comparison:

> **A booking record can show a reservation. A headcount can show a planning estimate. Neither, alone, confirms consent, accessibility needs, or authority to make a public commitment.**

“Could we simply publish the itinerary and ask people to correct it?” someone asked.

The Community Outreach Officer shook her head. “That makes the people named on it responsible for repairing our uncertainty. We should invite corrections privately, and only from people who choose to respond.”

The Events & Tours Coordinator drew a line through the word **confirmed** on the draft. She replaced it with **proposed** and added a note that the route, venue, capacity and accessibility arrangements still needed review. No one called the plan an approved event. It was an outline awaiting the right checks.

The Legal Journalist’s desk had received a headline, not yet a checked source packet. A second item concerned Malaysia and skilled-worker pathways. The words **Malaysia** and **work** had caught someone’s eye because the deployed team’s supplied geographic position was Kuala Lumpur. “So we can use it to shape the questions,” the Booking Agent said, “not to claim the answers.”

“Exactly,” said the Coordinator. “We ask the responsible people to check the actual place.”

The Community Outreach Officer returned to the guest list. She proposed a short, neutral message asking invitees to confirm only what they wished to share, including any access arrangements they wanted considered. The Booking Agent kept the reservation status tentative. The Coordinator left the route unapproved until the venue and safety checks could be completed.

Edliex had one more observation. The three documents did not disagree about the event. They described different things: a possible reservation, a planning estimate, and a request that had not yet been confirmed. Their mistake would have been to make them answer a question none of them had been written to answer.

By lunchtime, the papers had not produced a complete guest list or a finished itinerary. They had produced a better handover: what could be planned now, what needed consent, and which decisions belonged to someone else.

The label marked **PENDING** rested on the edge of the draft—not as a verdict on any guest, but as a reminder about the plan.
