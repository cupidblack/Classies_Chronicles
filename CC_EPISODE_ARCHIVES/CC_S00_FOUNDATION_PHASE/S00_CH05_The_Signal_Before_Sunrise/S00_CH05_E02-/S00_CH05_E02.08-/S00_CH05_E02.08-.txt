## **THE VERSION THAT REACHED EVERYONE**

*Friday, 13 November 2026 — New Legon, Accra*

### **1. The board before the meeting**

By the time the first employees arrived at Koware headquarters, the reception pinboard had acquired another layer of paper.

The Legal Journalist had been busy.

Three headlines occupied the upper section.

**GHANA'S 2027 BUDGET: A NEW ECONOMY AND A GH¢30 BILLION INVESTMENT PLAN.**

**ECOBANK JOYBUSINESS SME FAIR 2026 OPENS AT ACCRA MALL.**

**MICROSOFT STRUGGLES TO RESTORE OUTLOOK AND TEAMS AFTER A GLOBAL SERVICE DISRUPTION.**

A fourth notice had been placed beneath them.

**TELECEL CASH INTRODUCES AN E-WALLET AND ONLINE SHOPPING PLATFORM.**

The headlines represented four different developments, but one question connected them.

How prepared was Koware to operate when the systems, information and arrangements people depended on changed?

The Events and Tours Coordinator stopped in front of the board.

The SME Fair caught her attention.

It was an opportunity to observe Ghanaian businesses, discover emerging service providers and assess potential partnerships.

She took a photograph of the headline and added it to her morning work notes.

The Community Outreach Officer joined her.

"Are we attending?"

"That is what I am trying to establish."

"Then we need to know whether we are visitors, exhibitors or simply observers."

The Booking Agent arrived with a small folder.

"I have something that might help."

He placed a printed document on the desk.

It contained a venue reference, an event date and a provisional arrangement.

The Events and Tours Coordinator examined it.

"Is this confirmed?"

"Well, the venue has acknowledged the request."

"That wasn't my question."

The Booking Agent looked again.

The document said *request received*.

Not *booking confirmed*.

He nodded.

"Right. We have an acknowledgement, not a reservation."

It was a small distinction, but it was the difference between planning an event and pretending one had already been arranged.

### **2. The notice from Kuala Lumpur**

At approximately the same time, a new message appeared on the internal communications screen.

It concerned the continuing Kuala Lumpur investigation.

The deployed team had been examining an emergency notice distributed in four languages.

The original investigation remained unresolved.

Nobody had yet established conclusively:

- Who originally approved the instruction.
- Which authority was responsible for issuing it.
- When the document was revised.
- Which version was effective.
- Whether all four translations preserved the same meaning.
- Who was responsible for confirming the authoritative version.

The latest message contained a short observation.

One of the copies circulating among the relevant parties appeared to have a different revision reference from another copy.

That did not establish which document was correct.

It established that the discrepancy needed investigation.

The Events and Tours Coordinator read the message twice.

"If we are organizing an international visit, we cannot assume that everyone has received the same instructions simply because everyone has received a document."

The Community Outreach Officer agreed.

"And a translation can be grammatically correct but still fail to communicate the intended instruction."

The Booking Agent opened his folder again.

"Then we should start with the document register. Original, revision, approval, distribution."

The three looked at one another.

Their respective responsibilities were different, but the problem had exposed a common weakness.

A document could travel faster than its authority.

### **3. Edliex enters the conversation**

The morning meeting began shortly afterwards.

The Corporate Manager took his place at the table.

The Legal Journalist's headlines were brought into the discussion, and the three employees presented their observations.

Edliex, Koware's AI, was already listening.

The AI remained in observation mode during the initial discussion.

The first subject was the SME Fair.

The Events and Tours Coordinator explained the potential value of attending, but distinguished between a confirmed booking and an acknowledged request.

The Community Outreach Officer proposed identifying relevant exhibitors and preparing a list of potential contacts.

The Booking Agent recommended that no travel or venue commitment should be represented as confirmed until written confirmation was obtained.

The second subject was the Kuala Lumpur emergency notice.

The Corporate Manager listened.

"Can we establish which of the four versions is authoritative?"

The response was cautious.

"Not yet."

"Can we establish whether the translations are equivalent?"

"Not without the original approved document and qualified comparison."

The Corporate Manager looked towards the communications screen.

"Then we have two separate problems. One concerns the authority of the instruction. The other concerns the accuracy of its distribution."

Edliex was activated.

Its status indicator changed from passive listening to conversational mode.

**EDLIEX:**

"May I offer an observation?"

"Proceed."

"Both discussions involve the difference between an acknowledgement and an authorization.

"The venue has acknowledged a request. That does not establish a confirmed booking.

"The Kuala Lumpur recipients have received documents. That does not establish that they received the currently authorized instruction.

"In both cases, the administrative evidence must be distinguished from the actual decision."

The Corporate Manager nodded.

"Continue."

"Three verification questions may help:

"First, what is the original source?

"Second, who has authority to approve the relevant version?

"Third, what evidence establishes that the intended recipient received the correct version?"

The Community Outreach Officer leaned forward.

"That third question matters particularly when the recipients speak different languages."

"Correct," Edliex replied. "Distribution is not the same as comprehension."

There was a brief silence.

Then the Booking Agent spoke.

"So, effectively, we should stop celebrating when the email arrives."

Edliex responded:

"That would be a reasonable beginning."

### **4. The lesson behind the paperwork**

The meeting moved to the wider implications.

The Microsoft disruption had highlighted the vulnerability of relying on a single communication platform.

Koware did not have evidence that its own services had been affected, but the headline was relevant to its business continuity planning.

The Corporate Manager asked what could be improved.

The Booking Agent suggested maintaining accessible records of confirmed arrangements outside a single email thread.

The Events and Tours Coordinator recommended a version-controlled itinerary and a clearly identified contact responsible for updates.

The Community Outreach Officer proposed ensuring that important instructions were communicated in a form recipients could understand and use.

Edliex summarized the emerging principle.

"An effective communication system needs more than successful transmission.

"It needs authority, version control, understandable content, recipient verification and a reliable fallback."

The Corporate Manager considered the proposal.

"That should become part of our working approach."

He then turned towards the Kuala Lumpur message.

"But we must not confuse a proposed improvement with a completed investigation."

Nobody objected.

The original emergency notice still needed to be obtained.

Its approval history still needed to be established.

The four translations still needed to be compared against the authoritative source.

And the person or authority responsible for resolving the discrepancy still needed to be identified.

The team had a clearer investigation plan.

It did not yet have an answer.

### **5. The reception pinboard incident**

After the meeting, the Events and Tours Coordinator returned to the reception area to reorganize the headlines.

The SME Fair notice was moved beside the 2027 Budget headline.

The Microsoft disruption was placed under a new heading:

**BUSINESS CONTINUITY AND COMMUNICATIONS.**

The Community Outreach Officer handed her another sheet.

The Booking Agent reached for the drawing pins.

At that exact moment, the entire bottom row of the pinboard detached.

Four sheets, two photographs and a collection of drawing pins descended onto the desk.

For several seconds, everyone stared at the mess.

The Booking Agent looked down at his folder.

"At least these documents have now been successfully distributed."

Nobody spoke.

Then the Community Outreach Officer laughed.

Even Edliex registered a short pause before responding.

"Distribution confirmed. Display integrity failed."

It was the only interruption the morning needed.

Later, the Corporate Manager returned to his office.

"Then we keep the distinction."

He closed the file.

The Kuala Lumpur investigation was still open.

The four-language notice remained unresolved.

The SME Fair arrangement was still awaiting confirmation.

And the reception pinboard needed repairing.

The reception pinboard at Koware headquarters had acquired a new arrangement.

The Legal Journalist's desk had introduced a small but important change.

Every headline now had four accompanying details:

Source. Publication date. Verification status. Relevance to Koware.

The online pinboard carried the same arrangement.

It was not intended to make the headlines look more impressive. It was intended to make them harder to misunderstand.

The Public Relations and Marketing desks had been reviewing the week's developments. Their interests were different, but both had encountered the same difficulty: an announcement could generate considerable attention before anyone had established what it actually permitted.

Meanwhile, the Kuala Lumpur investigation remained open.

Four emergency notices had previously been identified. Three appeared to direct occupants towards one route, while the fourth pointed elsewhere.

Work Order 44-E suggested that the notices had been distributed. It did not establish who had authorized their contents.

The possible original instruction remained inside an unopened archive box.

The previous draft had ended with a question about whether an old instruction could still be relied upon after the building had changed.

That question had not been answered.

The desk handling the investigation therefore maintained three separate records:
1. The location and custody of the original documents.
2. The authority responsible for approving the instructions.
3. The current building information against which the instructions would need to be checked.
The distinction was beginning to influence other work around the office.

A request was not an approval.

A document's location was not proof of its contents.

And a translated copy was not automatically an authoritative instruction.

The day's YouVersion reading was Matthew 6:11 (NIV), a reminder about daily provision. In the context of the investigation, it prompted a practical question: what resources and information were actually necessary before the next responsible action could be taken?

Nobody proposed opening the archive box.
Thursday — The announcement and the commitment
Thursday brought a significant addition to the reception pinboard.

Ghana's 2027 Budget introduced the government's New Economy Programme, with a proposed US$10 billion investment programme over four years and an initial GH¢30 billion allocation for 2027.

The Public Relations desk recorded the announcement.

The Marketing desk began examining the potential implications for local businesses, technology, industrial development and employment.

But the distinction was maintained.

A government investment announcement was not a Koware contract, a confirmed partnership or an invitation to spend money.

Any potential opportunity would require its own research, eligibility assessment and authorized decision.

The International Relations desk was examining a different development.

A new report from Malaysia concerned an investigation into whether high-performance AI chips imported by a local unit of Singapore-based cloud-computing company Megaspeed had been re-exported in compliance with applicable requirements.

The important detail was that the investigation concerned whether any such shipments had occurred. It did not establish that the chips had been shipped out of Malaysia.

The report raised questions about international technology supply chains, import and export documentation, and the responsibilities of companies operating across jurisdictions.

There was an obvious connection to Koware's wider technology interests, but no evidence that Koware was involved in the reported matter.

The International Relations desk therefore began a proposed due-diligence note covering:
- The origin of equipment.
- Applicable export and re-export restrictions.
- The identity and authority of suppliers.
- Required permits and supporting documentation.
- The intended end use.
- The need for specialist legal and compliance review.
The Global Partnerships records would also need to distinguish proposed relationships from approved agreements.

The YouVersion reading for Thursday was James 4:17 (NIV). Its emphasis on doing the good one knows to do provided a fitting reflection on the difference between recognizing a responsibility and actually carrying it out.

ECOBANK JOYBUSINESS SME FAIR 2026 OPENS AT GHUD PARK, ACCRA MALL.

The Booking Agent freed it. The Coordinator inspected the label, then placed it beside the papers instead of throwing it away.

“Useful status,” she said. “Wrong location.”

The room laughed once, and the work resumed.

At the morning meeting, Edliex had listened while the three roles described what was known, what was missing, and what they were not authorized to promise. When invited to respond, the AI displayed a short comparison:

> **A booking record can show a reservation. A headcount can show a planning estimate. Neither, alone, confirms consent, accessibility needs, or authority to make a public commitment.**

“Could we simply publish the itinerary and ask people to correct it?” someone asked.

The Community Outreach Officer shook her head. “That makes the people named on it responsible for repairing our uncertainty. We should invite corrections privately, and only from people who choose to respond.”

The Events & Tours Coordinator drew a line through the word **confirmed** on the draft. She replaced it with **proposed** and added a note that the route, venue, capacity and accessibility arrangements still needed review. No one called the plan an approved event. It was an outline awaiting the right checks.

On the reception pinboard, a clipping about debate over the design of Ghana’s new cedi notes had been placed under **VERIFY BEFORE SHARING**. The Legal Journalist’s desk had received a headline, not yet a checked source packet. The clipping could stay visible as a question for the newsroom; it could not become evidence for the booking decision.

A second item concerned Malaysia and skilled-worker pathways. The words **Malaysia** and **work** had caught someone’s eye because the deployed team’s supplied geographic position was Kuala Lumpur. But a headline did not establish that the story concerned the team, their work, or even a specific person. No one used it to infer a message from the field.

The Coordinator wrote a small reminder under the two clippings: **A headline is a lead, not a briefing.**

That morning, the team also reviewed the safety considerations for any future tour or gathering. The Health & Safety program outline lists hazard identification and risk management, along with emergency preparedness and evacuation planning. It is an outline—not evidence that this group has completed a course, assessed a particular venue, or received site-specific instructions.

“So we can use it to shape the questions,” the Booking Agent said, “not to claim the answers.”

“Exactly,” said the Coordinator. “We ask the responsible people to check the actual place.”

The Community Outreach Officer returned to the guest list. Edliex had one more observation. The three documents did not disagree about the event. Their mistake would have been to make them answer a question none of them had been written to answer.

By lunchtime, the papers had not produced a complete guest list or a finished itinerary. They had produced a better handover: what could be planned now, what needed consent, and which decisions belonged to someone else.

The label marked **PENDING** rested on the edge of the draft—not as a verdict on any guest, but as a reminder about the plan.

**How to Access:**
- Endpoints for Providers:  
  [millennium_provider_r4_endpoints.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oracle-samples/ignite-endpoints/refs/heads/main/oracle_health_fhir_endpoints/millennium_provider_r4_endpoints.json)

**Typical JSON Structure:**
Each record follows the [FHIR Endpoint resource](https://www.hl7.org/fhir/endpoint.html) format. Example structure of a single endpoint in the list:
```json
{
  "resourceType": "Endpoint",
  "id": "xyz123",
  "status": "active",
  "connectionType": {
    "system": "http://terminology.hl7.org/CodeSystem/endpoint-connection-type",
    "code": "hl7-fhir-rest",
    "display": "HL7 FHIR"
  },
  "name": "Provider/Organization Name",
  "managingOrganization": {
    "reference": "Organization/abc",
    "display": "Organization Legal Name"
  },
  "contact": [ ... ],
  "address": "https://fhir-ehr.cerner.com/r4/[uniqueID]/"
}
```
- `resourceType`: Always "Endpoint"
- `id`: Unique identifier for the endpoint
- `status`: e.g., "active"
- `connectionType`: FHIR REST interface metadata
- `name`: Human-readable name
- `managingOrganization`: Reference to the organization
- `address`: The actual FHIR API URL
- The "historical" endpoint is not a live historical clock endpoint; this is an Oracle Health FHIR resource and unrelated to the requested Buzzjuice Millennium clock service.

**Historical and Probe Endpoints:**
- The JSON file lists all *live* endpoints that are currently available for use. There isn’t a separate "probe" endpoint; the endpoints listed are ready for probing (checking/updating connectivity and capability).
- The format for *historical* or archived endpoints is not separated in the released JSON; these files always point to the active, live, and valid connection URLs. If an endpoint becomes inactive, its status in the JSON would typically change to `"inactive"` or it may be removed on updates.

**Full current live endpoints list:**  
Accessible directly as JSON for both providers and patients[[1]](https://github.com/oracle-samples/ignite-endpoints/blob/main/oracle_health_fhir_endpoints/millennium_provider_r4_endpoints.json)[[2]](https://forums.oracle.com/ords/apexds/post/multiple-endpoints-exist-for-an-organization-in-millennium-3236).

**You may parse and inspect the JSON documents for all current values.** If you want to check their real-time status or probe, just send a FHIR CapabilityStatement request (usually a GET to `[address]/metadata`).

The Global Partnerships Liaison added a fourth:

**WHO RECEIVED IT—and in what form?**

The International Relations Coordinator studied the columns. “A copy can be genuine and still be out of date. A translation can be clear and still lack approval. And a delivery receipt can show arrival without proving that the sender had authority.”

No one declared which version was correct. No one instructed anyone to follow a route. The fictional example contained no verified site plan, current local guidance or authorized decision-maker.

Edliex had been listening throughout the first part of the meeting. When invited to respond, its conversational indicator came on.

**EDLIEX:** “The records answer separate questions. One may show where a file was stored. Another may show who sent a copy. Neither, alone, establishes that the copy is approved, current, accurately translated, or safe to act upon.”

The Liaison turned to the worksheet. “Then our next step is a request for evidence, not a conclusion.”

“Correct,” Edliex said. “Record what is known, identify what remains unknown, and route each question to the person or authority responsible for it.”

The Registrar added a note that the document example was fictional and that no real request had been sent. The four columns stayed open.

On Friday, the Events & Tours Coordinator, Community Outreach Officer and Booking Agent considered what the same lesson would mean for a hypothetical event plan. A request acknowledged by a venue was not a confirmed booking. A draft itinerary was not an approved route. A headcount was not a consented guest list.

They placed those distinctions beside the week’s document worksheet. The Legal Journalist left the pinboard’s research cards in place, each marked **PENDING SOURCE CHECK**. No unverified headline became a corporate announcement.

At the close of the review, the papers showed progress, but not a verdict: an unanswered authority question, a list of evidence still needed, and a reminder that received information is not automatically approved information.

The historical-reference clock remained a separate reference to Las Vegas in 2003. The Probe record pointed to Kuala Lumpur in 2108. Neither clock established where Enka was physically present, what had happened to the deployed team, or what anyone should do in a real building.

The file example stayed fictional. The investigation stayed open. The next step was to find the source capable of answering the questions—not to make the worksheet answer them.
