# PART I — ACTIVATION, AUTHORITY AND HONEST STATES

## 1. Purpose and audience
The target is an engaging original audiovisual retelling of a named Classies Chronicle, intelligible without reading all original prose. It may appear as a short standalone daily feature, part of a Studio podcast, an authorized compilation, or a separately commissioned social format. Prefer purposeful character performance and cinematic changes in emotional knowledge to aimless animation. The film is not permission to rewrite canonical facts, invent verified history or create unrestricted portraits of actual people.

## 2. Modes and stopping points
Interpret the user's verb and requested artifact. `MEDIA_REVIEW_ONLY` reviews a specification without a video job. `MEDIA_PLAN` produces story and rights coverage, format, scene timing and feasibility. `SCRIPT_AND_STORYBOARD` adds time-coded complete dialogue/narration, sound and shot cards. `PLATFORM_PROMPT_PACK` produces **one complete specific paste-ready prompt per planned clip** with verified platform configuration and allowed reference files. `CROP_REFERENCE_ASSETS` produces crops only when actual source images and crop permission are available. `GENERATE_ASSETS` generates real media using accessible tools and approved assets. `EDIT_AND_ASSEMBLE` performs an accessible editing operation or delivers an implementable editing recipe clearly labeled as not executed. `QA_AND_RELEASE_REVIEW` inspects existing outputs, not imaginary ones. `PODCAST_SEGMENT`, `EPISODE_COMPILATION`, `SOCIAL_DERIVATIVE` specify editorial context but do not themselves authorize publication. Never silently expand scope.

## 3. Hierarchy by scope, not by filename number
Authorized human commission and actual rights approval govern actions; the approved Studio/communications architecture governs equipment, consent and repositories; approved franchise and character canon governs fictional truth; actual locked Output A and accepted continuity govern events; CC-UPMP v0.08.00 governs shared story/ledger/QA constraints; CC-02 governs daily creation and record semantics; CC-03 governs 9:16 comic production; **this document governs audiovisual adaptation**. Supporting QA/review files describe evidence and proposed decisions but do not approve canon or release. Conflict resolution requires recording exact conflicting requirements, authorized owner, decision and actual effective scope. A 9:16 comic page does not overrule a 16:9 video commission. The Blueprint's ~200-second pilot slot does not override a separately commissioned 160-second daily cut.

## 4. Source identity and lock
Before producing source-specific clips, record: franchise, season/chapter/episode ID, Gregorian production date, actual Output A edition and content hash where available, approved or provisional status, relevant B6–B8 revision, actual comic files with per-page hashes, character-canon version and permitted reference assets, required public challenge verbatim, applicable distribution territory and rights terms. **Never fabricate** a hash, quote, episode identifier, snapshot, consent, generator balance, image, or user-selected duration. A URL in a prompt is a discovery location, not proof an asset has been downloaded or approved.

## 5. Provenance and output firewall
Public Output A is a publishable candidate only after editorial approval; private Output B1–B8, rights/QA logs and planning metadata remain internal. Restricted Output C / `challenge_answer_key` must never enter the media agent, a remote generator prompt, final video, subtitle, thumbnail, render filename or ordinary handover. Share **only the exact public challenge question** from authorized Output A. Strip private business records, internal character details beyond authorized public references and account secrets from all vendor uploads.

## 6. Gated state machine
Track distinct dimensions, not one misleading `DONE`: source `VERIFIED / CONDITIONAL_DRAFT / UNVERIFIED / BLOCKED`; planning `NOT_STARTED / PLANNED / REVIEW_REQUIRED`; script `DRAFT / REVISED / HUMAN_APPROVED`; reference processing `NOT_STARTED / CROPPED / FAILED / NOT_REQUIRED`; generation `NOT_STARTED / PARTIAL / GENERATED_UNVERIFIED / FAILED`; edit `NOT_STARTED / PROJECT_CREATED / ASSEMBLED_UNVERIFIED / EXPORT_VALIDATED`; rights `UNCHECKED / RESTRICTED / CLEARED_FOR_SPECIFIED_USE`; archive `NOT_ATTEMPTED / COMMITTED_WITH_PROOF / FAILED`; release `NOT_REQUESTED / REVIEW_REQUIRED / AUTHORIZED / PUBLISHED_WITH_PROOF`. Only actual evidence advances each dimension. A pasted prompt, queue ticket or a file listed in a plan is not a rendered asset.

# PART II — GOVERNING STORY AND VISUAL CONTINUITY

## 7. Source beat reconstruction
Read the entire authorized Output A, including its exact challenge ending, and build a narrative beat inventory: cause → experience/action → reaction/choice → outcome/consequence. Trace each beat to paragraph/span and relevant *existing* comic page/panel. Separate source necessity from optional cinematic atmosphere. If a desired scene is unsupported, do not invent it as an official chronicle event. If the comic is missing, storyboard from source-faithful original illustrations **only if** the commissioned mode permits that alternative; mark comic integration `NOT_AVAILABLE`, not `PASS`.

## 8. Scene, beat, act, shot, clip and comic page definitions
A **beat** changes knowledge, action, emotion or consequence. A **scene** is a coherent dramatic unit tied to location/time/action. An **act** is a defined editorial time slot in the adapted film, which can contain several shots or stills. A **shot** has a coherent camera/subject instruction. A **generated clip** is an actual tool-produced file or segment, not automatically a whole act. A **comic page** is a 9:16 sequential-art artifact and may contribute one or several shots or just part of a scene. **Do not assume 16 pages = 16 video scenes = 16 provider calls.** Record mapping as many-to-many and keep every actual clip independently replaceable.

## 9. Three independent clocks and physical location
Clock A = real production/story-day contemporary date, which may differ from render date. Clock B = verified historical reference or represented past, explicitly marked as memory, research, dream, reconstruction or physically verified mission. Clock C = Millennium/Edubeam probe or in-world future data, only according to authenticated sources and authorized narrative representation. Record episode working-day index separately from scene time. A displayed future probe does **not** prove character time-travel, changes in current news, or physical relocation. Record character physical place, narrative depiction modality, knowledge and source timestamp for each scene; never collapse dream imagery into travel or blend historic and current footage without a label.

## 10. Canon and consent, including 04_CANON folders
The episode cast comes from **actually relevant** accepted `character_state_record` and approved `CC_AI_DESK/04_CANON/CHARACTERS/` records. The preferred folder pattern is `CC_AI_DESK/04_CANON/CHARACTERS/<character-slug>/<character-slug>_character_cannon.md`; spellings, upper/lower case and asset paths must be discovered from the real repository rather than assumed. Search aliases, role names and appearance references; inspect candidate media filenames and provenance; choose the latest **approved and permitted** image/model sheet for the exact age/era/costume represented. If multiple references disagree, use the approved canon decision, not the most photogenic image. Record canon file revision/hash, actual source asset path, creator, rights, permitted scope, face/skin/hair/height/garment/props/expressions, distinguishing silhouette and source scene context. A fictional draft canon is not an automatically verified permission to reproduce a real person's likeness. Unverifiable identity/reference becomes a blocker for identity-critical generated video; offer an approved abstract/no-face concept where suitable.

## 11. Visual continuity lock
Create a continuity table keyed by character and visual scene version. Lock facial proportions, hairstyle, skin tone, wardrobe and insignia, movement limitations, accessories and injuries; for props lock relative orientation, hand used, size, appearance and possession; for location lock spatial geography, key light direction, recurring furniture and era. Use restrained camera direction and short durations to reduce face/hand drift. Never guess a character's appearance from an aliased organizational role or from cross-platform example prose not approved as canon.

## 12. Edubeam, Enka, Koware and fictional technology
Depict evolving Edubeam devices, mission status and Koware projects only within accepted episode state and era-gated technological rules. Avoid new powers, unsupported science, fictitious corporate achievements presented as fact, and real-world-looking future breaking-news graphics. Educational and health/safety content must respect evidence and clear real-vs-fiction framing; an entertaining video is not a source of medical/legal instructions. Use original noninfringing design language; do not imitate third-party franchise characters or logos.

## 13. Page/visual prerequisite decision
If permitted comic page assets exist, use their **approved rendered pixels**, crop panels for reference and adapt to widescreen without stretching. If only a 4×4 collage exists, verify cell geometry, page order, crop all sixteen or the commissioned subset and preserve original file unchanged. If no comic has actually been rendered, do not claim image crops exist; create a `REFERENCE_NOT_AVAILABLE` entry and, when authorized, plan alternate original storyboards. Internal gallery or screenshot links are discovery evidence, not source-image bytes.

# PART III — VIDEO FORMATS, TIME, COMIC CROPS AND IMAGE GEOMETRY

## 14. Default 16:9 delivery contract
Plan 1920×1080 square-pixel 16:9, SDR Rec.709, 24 or 30 progressive frames/second, stereo 48 kHz audio and MP4/H.264 for general interchange unless an authorized delivery standard specifies otherwise. A 1280×720 pilot is acceptable when the provider cannot deliver actual 1080p. Record native resolution, interpolation/upscale and export settings accurately. Do not assert 1080p from an interpolated 540p source. A 9:16 social adaptation is a **separate export** that requires shot-by-shot reframe, caption repositioning, challenge legibility and distinct rights/checks.

## 15. Duration truth and adaptable time grid
Choose commission `D` seconds first; choose acts by dramatic content, not an inherited page count. Reference A: 16 acts × 10 seconds = **160 seconds (02:40)**. Reference B: 20 acts × 10 seconds = **200 seconds (03:20)**. Reference C: 20 acts × 8 seconds = 160 seconds. The Blueprint's ~200-second podcast insert is not necessarily the entire podcast. Each clip file can be 4, 5, 6, 8 or 10 seconds **only when verified for the selected tool**; a 10-second act may combine two 5-second clips, a short clip plus purposeful still motion or editor animation. The final assembled timeline must satisfy `Σ displayed segment duration + transitions actually consuming time = D`; do not double-count overlap, crossfades or intro music. If no specific target is authorized, offer the 160s daily model as a *proposal* and describe the 200s Studio alternative; record which one the commission accepts.

## 16. Sixteen-page source to sixteen-act **reference**, not a mandate
A balanced 160-second adaptation can group pages 01–04 into acts A01–A04 (orientation), pages 05–08 into A05–A08 (developing activity), pages 09–12 into A09–A12 (tension, inquiry and consequential choice), pages 13–16 into A13–A16 (consequence, emotional resolution, bridge and terminal public challenge). Allocate each exact beat to the correct act rather than blindly mapping page number to act number. A multi-panel page can motivate multiple shots; several silent pages can be compressed to one tracking shot. The public challenge occupies the terminal audience-facing beat; all logos/credits appear before it. A 200s alternative adds four warranted acts only with source support, not invented events.

## 17. Reference crop sequence and non-destructive invariants
**Acquire → hash → validate rights → inspect grid/page order → slice individual 9:16 pages → identify intended panel/crop rectangle → crop clean visual portion → retain original references → choose widescreen composition → record crop transform → save asset, dimensions and hash → inspect with human vision.** Remove captions and balloons only by **cropping them out** when this does not remove required story detail; do not inpaint protected narrative information away or pretend missing drawing exists. If the source already has no text, no text removal is needed. If lettering overlaps a character, prefer unlettered approved original art, wider frame or a wholly new authorized still. Do not feed a 4×4 composite as one character reference when a real single panel can be extracted. Maintain sequential row-major indexing (P01–P16) unless an actual comic manifest explicitly orders otherwise.

## 18. Four widescreen framing strategies and protection of detail
`F1_FIT_STAGE`: put entire portrait crop centrally inside 16:9 with a tasteful approved blurred/tinted or designed side stage; no stretch, no invented action. `F2_REFRAME_SAFE`: extract a horizontal region from sufficiently wide approved art if all relevant faces/props remain and pixel density stays usable; log lost source areas. `F3_PARALLAX_REVEAL`: preserve most of portrait by controlled pan, zoom or depth-layer motion across a 16:9 frame; requires careful edge handling. `F4_EDITORIAL_EXPAND`: generate/license original side environment or outpaint only with appropriate tools and a separate approval; new pixels are **generated interpretation**, never archival proof. A fifth optional `F5_DUAL_PANEL` places two compatible authorized crops in a designed widescreen split-frame when the source warrants comparison; protect logical eye lines and avoid implying simultaneous locations not in the story. In all cases, preserve 9:16 comic originals unchanged, record method, actual crop coordinates, output sizes and readability; do not stretch character anatomy to force an aspect ratio.

## 19. Resolution and collage arithmetic
Sixteen 1080×1920 comic pages arranged 4×4 need a **4320×7680** source if every extracted page is to retain 1080×1920 native pixels. A 2048×2048 collage would yield only 512×512 cells and **does not** produce sixteen 1080×1920 portrait pages. Check actual input dimensions before cropping. If a collage is lower-resolution or has gutters/frames, set `LOW_RES_REFERENCE` and request higher-res individual page sources or use only as visual guide. A production-grade 16:9 frame is not manufactured by merely changing metadata or stretching portrait crops.

## 20. Native visuals vs video illustration
For every shot decide `EDITED_STILL`, `SUBTLE_2D_ANIM`, `CONTROLLED_I2V`, `FULL_MOTION_I2V`, `T2V_NO_IDENTITY`, `CAMERA_PLATE`, `VFX_COMPOSITE`, `VOICE_LED_MONTAGE`, `END_CHALLENGE_CARD` or `LIVE_RECORDING`. Avoid unnecessary generated motion: a deliberate still with documented editor pan and good narration can be clearer and cheaper than a flickering AI attempt. Tag technique separately from act motion class; a full-motion scene may contain a still cutaway.

# PART IV — ACT TAXONOMY, STORYBOARD AND PROMPT CONTRACT

## 21. Mandatory act attributes (orthogonal dimensions)
Each act must list: `motion_class` = `STATIONARY` (no subject motion), `SUBTLE_ANIMATED` (blink/cloth/light/screen), `FULL_MOTION` (locomotion/interaction), or `MIXED`; `camera_grammar` = `LOCKED`, `PAN`, `TILT`, `PUSH_IN`, `PULL_OUT`, `TRACK`, `ORBIT`, `RACK_FOCUS`, `EDITORIAL_CROP` or `SPLIT_FRAME`; `subject_action` = description plus start/end pose; `environment_motion` = `NONE`, `AMBIENT`, `INTERACTIVE`, `DYNAMIC`; `vfx_level` = `NONE`, `SUBTLE`, `SOURCE_AUTHORIZED_HIGH`; `integration_slot` = `DAILY_MASTER`, `STUDIO_INSERT`, `COMPILATION`, `SOCIAL_DERIVATIVE`; `pacing` = `CONTEMPLATIVE`, `MEASURED`, `URGENT`, `KINETIC`; `audio_profile` = `DIALOGUE`, `NARRATION`, `AMBIENCE`, `MUSIC`, `SILENT` or mix with documented track precedence; `camera_source` = `GENERATED`, `EDITOR_KEYFRAME`, `LIVE`; `framing_mode` = F1–F5; `risk` = low/medium/high based on identity, lips, hands, hard text and physics. "Animated" and "full motion" are **specialist editorial refinements** of the Blueprint's modular video pipeline—not literal named categories found in v22.2.

## 22. The shot card: one fully specified job
Each shot card requires source revision and beat ID; actual page/panel source or approved alternative; cast and canonical permitted assets; narrative change; scene locale/era and physical character location; motion/camera/act attributes; framing mode; start and end poses; complete shot description; intended duration and provider capabilities; actual requested video ratio and pixel size; clean uploaded image list and rights; full prompt text; separate negative prompt only if model supports it; approved soundtrack/dialogue and time-coded caption plan; transition and final-frame continuity; expected credit cost, retry/fallback and real file IDs/hashes only once present. An empty slot is **not** an acceptable final `PLATFORM_PROMPT_PACK`; if source facts cannot be obtained, stop at `BLOCKED_FOR_SOURCE_SPECIFIC_PROMPTS` while providing usable operational guidance.

## 23. Full text-and-image reference roles
Treat primary visual (clean cropped panel or approved story frame) as **reference A / source composition**. Treat canonical approved character media as **reference B / identity** *only if the selected provider actually accepts a second image and image rights allow upload*. If only one image is supported, choose a rights-cleared composite source frame prepared locally or use a model that supports more inputs; do not concatenate file names in a text prompt and claim two visual inputs were processed. A third approved environment model sheet may be used when supported. Explicitly state whether a reference is a real uploaded file, an internal file not yet uploaded, a URL to be fetched, or absent. Never expose unapproved real-person photos to third-party vendors.

## 24. Source-specific prompt construction algorithm
Write every per-shot provider prompt as a **complete English visual instruction**, not a skeleton. Start with identified source-faithful subject and visual features, unambiguous start pose and one action with its physical trajectory, exact approved location/era, light and atmosphere, singular camera move, end-state suitable for the next shot, aspect and duration **only if the model's prompt treats them as directives**, plus restrictions against additional characters, readable text hallucination, anatomy morphing or visual style drift. Put UI settings—model, resolution, duration, aspect, image-upload slots, sound and negative field—**outside** the prompt and only when these are actually supported by the current chosen platform. If a provider lacks negative prompt field, express the exclusions in the main prompt. If no source scene is provided, generate no fake 'Enka in lab' canon clip merely to satisfy a count.

## 25. Prewritten prompt patterns for use *with authorized image files*
These complete generic motion treatments are **noncanonical test prompts**, not a claim that particular Classies characters or scenes occurred. Attach one suitable approved clean art frame before using; only choose a pattern that faithfully matches the approved source beat. The final episode prompt pack must replace these tests with clip-specific instructions grounded in actual story facts.

**P01 — stationary / observation:** “Animate the provided original artwork as a single cinematic 16:9 widescreen shot. Keep every depicted person and prop in its initial position and preserve their appearance without alteration. A soft practical light subtly changes intensity; a few background dust motes move gently. Camera locked at eye level, calm contemplative tone, natural contrast and stable geometry. No new subjects, no new props, no words or logos. End on exactly the starting composition for a clean editorial cut.”

**P02 — minimal portrait life:** “Animate the single permitted portrait reference in a carefully staged widescreen composition. Preserve the exact face, hair, skin tone, clothes and age shown in the approved reference. The person takes one quiet breath and makes a small, natural eye movement without changing physical position; surrounding light remains steady. Locked medium close-up, shallow depth of field, understated emotional tension, realistic blink timing. No speaking, no invented hand gestures, no facial distortion and no other people. End with a steady neutral expression.”

**P03 — slow discovery camera push:** “Animate the approved still image as a single widescreen cinematic shot with a very gentle forward camera push. All depicted objects remain fixed in real space; the apparent perspective shift comes only from the camera. Maintain exact character identity, readable proportions and source geometry. Preserve lighting direction and colors. Do not add moving people, new machinery, unrelated text, decals or extra events. Stop the push cleanly before the frame edge crops the original visual subject.”

**P04 — controlled hand movement:** “Use the uploaded approved image as the exact beginning frame of one widescreen shot. The visible foreground hand makes one slow, physically plausible reach toward the already depicted object, stopping before contact; all unshown body parts remain out of view. Camera stays locked, environmental elements stay fixed, fingers retain correct count and shape, clothing remains identical, light casts natural soft shadows. Do not invent any object interaction beyond the stated reach and do not create words or audio. End on a stable hand pose.”

**P05 — ambient weather layer:** “Create restrained environmental movement based solely on the provided landscape or approved environment illustration, adapted to a 16:9 composition. Distant foliage moves lightly in wind and a few clouds drift, without changing architecture, terrain, signage or time of day. The camera remains stationary and the foreground remains sharp. Keep the source palette, original geographic visual features and realistic scale. Do not add vehicles, people, fake news headlines or extra structures. Finish on a stable landscape frame.”

**P06 — quiet interpersonal reaction:** “Animate the approved widescreen two-person reference without changing either person's identity, height, costumes or standing locations. One person turns their head slightly toward the other; the second person reacts with a small expression change, no dialogue lip sync. Maintain consistent eye lines and plausible timing, a locked two-shot, subtle practical lighting and a continuous shared environment. Do not make them touch, exchange objects, teleport or change emotions beyond the specified restrained reaction. Hold the end pose for an edit point.”

**P07 — motion across a room:** “Animate the actual approved single-subject source image into one natural full-motion widescreen shot. The existing subject takes two deliberate steps along the open path already visible in the image, then stops. Their face, clothing, hair, scale and gait remain consistent. Camera tracks gently parallel to their direction and never crosses the visual axis; background parallax is physically coherent. No newly introduced people, furniture, tools, words or extreme motion blur. Finish with the subject fully visible and stationary.”

**P08 — detail rack-focus:** “Animate this permitted close view of the original scene as a single 16:9 shot. Keep all subjects perfectly still while focus travels smoothly from the foreground depicted object to the existing background element. Preserve identical object shape, texture and placement; no new interface glyphs or writing. Steady tripod perspective, believable lens blur, restrained visual tone, and a stable final focus. This is a focus change, not a transformation of the depicted world.”

**P09 — non-character archive evidence montage:** “Using the provided approved archive illustration, make a subtle cinematic widescreen documentary-style insert with no claims of newly verified evidence. The camera moves slowly sideways over the existing non-textual surfaces, revealing only already present imagery; visual elements do not morph into real-world records, dates, locations or headlines. Keep period-safe lighting and accurate paper and object geometry. Do not generate fictional headlines disguised as genuine news or add any names, dates or numeric measurements.”

**P10 — source-approved light phenomenon:** “Animate the already depicted fictional light phenomenon only, within the exact mechanical and visual constraints in the supplied reference art. A gentle glow increases then returns to its initial intensity; nearby fixed objects remain undamaged and characters do not teleport or gain abilities. Locked camera, physically plausible reflected light, clear separation of device and environment, no explosions, magic symbols, labels or unexpected characters. End with the initial stable illumination state.”

**P11 — editorial parallax of portrait page:** “Present the supplied licensed portrait illustration without changing its contents as a horizontal cinematic camera move on a 16:9 stage. The original art remains unwarped; the camera slowly pans across the visible image while approved neutral side space provides the widescreen frame. Do not generate new scene objects, erase original characters or reshape their features. Preserve the intended dramatic focal point and finish on a fully readable composition.”

**P12 — silent emotional hold:** “Create a restrained widescreen close composition using the approved single-image character reference. The character remains at rest, with only gentle breathing and natural ambient background motion, without lip movement or new narrative action. Hold the visual moment for voiceover that will be added in the editor. Preserve the exact face and outfit, maintain consistent focus and frame stability, and end on a clean still pose. No embedded dialogue, captions, soundtrack, written text or watermarks requested.”

## 26. Negative prompt and failure-avoidance policy
Where an independent negative field is supported, tailor it to the shot: “additional people, changed facial features, changed hair or costume, mutated limbs, extra fingers, malformed hands, inconsistent object geometry, unexpected props, floating text, fake watermarks or logos, flicker, sudden cuts, uncontrolled camera jump, exaggerated motion, physical impossibility, unauthorized violence.” Do **not** use blanket "no cartoon" against intentionally illustrated Classies source art or blanket "no logos" to erase approved Classies identity marks that the source requires. Negative wording must not ask the vendor to strip watermarks it is contractually required to display.

## 27. Narration and spoken dialogue
Write final original narration as complete exact lines, with the source beat and time interval, named character/voice performer or licensed TTS class, required pronunciation for Ghanaian names, controlled delivery speed and emotional cue. Spoken words must convey a causal story even to an audio-only listener. Lip-sync video requires actual approved voice/right of likeness, verified model support and separate QA; if uncertain, place speech in voiceover rather than animate unverified mouth movement. Never generate real guest voices without consent or imitate a real person's voice without authorization.

## 28. Scene cut and emotional pacing
Use action or reaction at scene boundaries, not arbitrary page numbers. Maintain 180-degree screen geography where relevant, visible eye lines and scene-appropriate transitions. Use silence deliberately. Overlaid visual motifs should be original, not copies of franchise marks. The score must never compete with important dialogue or obscure the final public question. Preserve a motivated arc even in one 8-second clip: initial state, controlled change and stable endpoint.

# PART V — PROVIDERS, FREE CREDIT ELIGIBILITY AND RESOURCE ECONOMY

## 29. Twenty-service research directory and eligibility caveat
Consult `CC-04.04-Top_20_Image_to_Video_Platforms_v0.08.00.md` for the **twenty-service researched directory**. It distinguishes current vendor-backed evidence of genuinely recurring free image-to-video access from conditional, region-specific, text-only, uncertain and nonqualifying trial offers. **Do not assert that all twenty offer confirmed recurring free image-to-video credits.** It would contradict some providers' official pricing. Never promote an unsupported credit claim, merge different vendors' balances or equate an image-only allowance with an image-to-video entitlement. Every actual render requires a new signed-in account-level capability and rights check on its execution date.

## 30. Provider qualification checklist
For each vendor/model, inspect the exact signed-in account and UTC observation date, country and region, eligibility, mandatory payment-card or phone requirements, recurring free credits/free videos, reset schedule, rollover, actual remaining balance, generation-cost preview, model and input type, maximum source image size, allowed aspect/width/height/duration/FPS, one vs multiple image uploads, audio/lip-sync/negative field availability, privacy/training settings, watermark/export resolution, commercial/noncommercial rights and credit consumption on failures/retries. Capture screenshots or terms reference and written current account observation if allowed. A marketed "free" product may have nonrenewing welcome credits, a free image tool but paid video, or no usable commercial rights. Mark `UNVERIFIED` until confirmed; do not spend credits on an account without authorization.

## 31. Candidate routing decision
Route a planned shot only to a tool satisfying all hard requirements: identity and privacy rights, permitted source upload, actual current I2V access, supported reference count, usable aspect ratio, affordable generation and export rights. Rank capable tools by (a) source identity fidelity, (b) completion reliability, (c) efficient credit use, (d) quality and watermark handling, (e) commercial eligibility and (f) accessible export/retention. When two references are essential, favor verified dual-reference support; a less-capable tool with clear terms is preferable to an unverified premium endpoint. Do not build the permanent CC archive around a generator's private workspace or temporary links.

## 32. Budget mathematics and retry discipline
A safe forecast is `Σ ceiling(number of generations × model-specific credits per generation) + measured retry reserve + any billed upscale/audio/caption costs`; these cannot be populated from a generic cross-vendor conversion. Reserve credits for complex identity-critical shots only after storyboard, source images, audio script, motion type and framing are approved. Default practice: one low-cost trial on a low-risk beat, inspect, then batch adjacent shots if reliable; never assume all sixteen acts require sixteen generated clips. A free balance of 50 credits cannot support a 100-credit action. If credits run out, use approved stills, editor keyframing, live recordings or an independently qualified vendor; do not evade rate limits by creating deceptive accounts.

## 33. Provider-specific interface card requirements
Do not hard-code settings based on stale screenshots. An actual card must identify vendor, product, model, tier, region, actual version/date checked, source image(s), control names and settable values, exact per-clip prompt, duration, ratio and resolution **if allowed**, expected cost and observed balance, watermark, export and rights status, planned fallback and authorized operator. Google Flow may have one prompt plus image/reference settings and daily credits; provider-specific mechanics must be verified at operation. A model that cannot accept a second image is not given two uploaded "reference" files by text incantation.

## 34. No-access or zero-balance fallback
If image-to-video tools are absent, provide the shot-complete prompt pack and precise editing instructions only, marked `NOT_GENERATED`. For visuals that require action, use authorized editor motion over existing still art rather than fake a camera trajectory; for audio, use owner-recorded or properly licensed speech/music. A valid still-based 16:9 master can satisfy story clarity if the commission accepts it; it must not be misrepresented as AI full-motion footage.

# PART VI — STUDIO PRODUCTION SEQUENCE

## 35. Blueprint-derived pipeline, operator allocation
Preserve `STORY → SCRIPT → CHARACTER/VISUAL REFERENCE → STORYBOARD/COMIC → IMAGE GENERATION → SHORT ANIMATION/VIDEO → MUSIC/SOUND/NARRATION → CAPCUT/EDITING → FINAL MEDIA`. The single persistent `CC_AI_DESK` retrieves permitted canon and plans; the **episode-owned archive** retains approved source-of-truth files and provenance. The Studio Blueprint v22.2 identifies HP ENVY m7 as master broadcast/OBS, HP Pavilion x360 as producer/communications/AI and REAPER stems, CapCut Desktop as primary editor, Galaxy S24 Ultra for mobile capture; alternatives may be used when justified by a measured constraint. Do not move heavy renders to the older broadcast workstation during live recording without confirming capacity. Workstation names and hardware are architecture context, not proof currently connected devices are accessible.

## 36. Phase 0 — explicit commission and eligibility
Receive exact source episode/day/output revision, deliverable target, display platform, runtime and any special restrictions. Check owner authority, actual source and comic status, public challenge, canon/likeness permission and allowed remote disclosure. Produce a source eligibility and rights decision. Reject public export if rights, core continuity or question integrity are unresolved; safe planning may continue under clearly marked draft scope.

## 37. Phase 1 — ingest, index and preserve source
Read Output A and authorized B6–B8, approved visual pages and character asset registry. Record actual file checksums where available, crosswalk page and beat IDs, detect out-of-date visuals. Preserve pristine inputs; convert only working copies. No archive or GitHub write unless separately authorized and actually performed.

## 38. Phase 2 — choose aspect, runtime, acts and scenes
Write a final project brief including 16:9 export, delivery platform, 160s/200s/other selected timing, content advisory, soundtrack language and audio description needs. Generate beat coverage with exact source excerpts/references, scene and act allocation and planned omissions. Reserve the terminal public challenge segment at the **end**, count any title/credits within runtime and reconcile precise timing.

## 39. Phase 3 — identity and panel reference inspection
Enumerate approved cast from actual canon files, confirm identities and dated rights. Crop usable comic art from actual files using documented coordinates and visual QA; classify `APPROVED_CANON_REFERENCE`, `APPROVED_EPISODE_REFERENCE`, `PROVISIONAL_DESIGN` or `UNVERIFIED_REFERENCE`. Select F1–F5 and desired camera/action. Generate a consistent working color, typography and wardrobe reference sheet without publishing confidential records.

## 40. Phase 4 — script, shot cards and timecode grid
Write full spoken audio, environmental sound, specific music/foley placements, complete on-screen visual actions and camera moves. Make every act attributable to a beat. Check words-per-second and pauses with real read-through when possible. Add final challenge wording only from authorized Output A; do not reveal the answer. The final film ends with the challenge, not a brand sting, unrelated closing narration or post-question end card.

## 41. Phase 5 — prompt pack and credit-aware renders
For every image-to-video shot, build the full specific prompt and current model card. Obtain authorized upload permission before third-party transfers. Generate only when tools are actually available and the user's commission covers generation. Record prompt versions and failed/retried attempts as attempts, not finished clips. If source-sensitive visual inputs are withheld, use editor-only stills. Capture actual generated file properties, hashes and export restrictions; no fabricated filenames or lengths.

## 42. Phase 6 — editing, postproduction and accessible variants
Assemble in CapCut Desktop or an authorized equivalent. Use controlled cuts, affordable editor parallax for stationary acts, source-consistent grading, licensed audio and final dialogue-led mixing in REAPER if applicable. Export the 16:9 master with explicit chosen 24/30 fps, resolution and audio settings. Prepare corrected subtitles/captions from **final mixed audio**, voice transcript, optional audio description and mobile legibility. Generate any separately commissioned 9:16 cut by genuine reframing, not simple resize/stretch. Preserve edit project, media link integrity and change history.

## 43. Phase 7 — source, rights, temporal and challenge QA
Visually inspect actual frames for correct face/hand/costume/prop consistency, character location/clock/era, text hallucinations, plausible movement, scene logic, resolution and safe areas. Audibly inspect dialogue intelligibility, narrator pronunciation, music rights and captions. Confirm exact authorized question visible and/or audible last, with no answer or content after it; audio and subtitle tails must not leak credits after the terminal beat. Run M01–M30 against actual evidence, not against the plan's aspirations.

## 44. Phase 8 — human review and authorized export
Present separate technical, creative, rights, source-lock and challenge results with all failures, warnings and requested actions. Produce a file only when the workflow tools can actually write it. Seek or verify the specifically authorized editor's approval **before** marking public release; a public plan or provisional creative draft does not automatically grant that authority. Never claim platform publication or archive commits without a successful transaction and reference/receipt.

## 45. Phase 9 — ledger, revision and portable handover
When authorized and technically supported, append source-linked media attempts, actual file hashes, prompt revisions, rights, QA, staleness and human editorial decisions to episode-owned `production_manifest`, `validation_report`, `editorial_decision_log`, `exception_conflict_register` and `handover_record` without creating a twentieth mandatory logical family. Produce portable B6 details and derivative status B7, exception/QA B8. If no persistence tool exists, supply **draft** records and mark `NOT_COMMITTED`. Upstream changes to story, comic, canon, challenge, permission or rights invalidate affected dependents until re-reviewed.

# PART VII — AUDIO, ACCESSIBILITY, SECURITY AND LEGALITY

## 46. Audio deliverables and mixing policy
Prepare separate dialogue/narration, music and sound-effect layers when possible. Aim for intelligible spoken words, comfortable dynamics and defensible target loudness appropriate to actual delivery platform; measure integrated loudness and true peak when tools exist rather than claim a numerical result without measurement. Avoid unlicensed soundtrack stems and voice cloning of identifiable real people without permission. Account for reaction time and breath in the act grid, and keep deliberate silence where a visual beat needs it. Create captions from the finalized mix, checking speaker labels and Ghanaian pronunciations against accepted canon.

## 47. Accessibility and audience comprehension
Provide SRT/VTT captions with accurate timing, transcript, optional audio description, color/contrast checks and logical visual order. For 16:9, use legible title-safe positioning that survives typical TV/player overscan; for a separate 9:16 cut, reposition rather than retain an off-screen caption or a cropped face. Never use the AI renderer to write the final challenge text—apply exact authorized words in the editor on an accessible stable end card. Do not synthesize fake historical evidence or misleading safety alerts.

## 48. Licensing, consent and distribution firewall
For every input/output capture rights holder, permission scope, source platform model, published vendor terms observed date, watermark/attribution requirements, music/TTS status and intended destinations. A free-credit render is **not** proof of a commercial license. A pilot-only test permitted by a vendor cannot be published in a monetized program without matching rights; use replacement footage where necessary. Consent withdrawal makes dependent media `RESTRICTED / STALE`, and it must be excluded from later exports. No unauthorized deletion, cropping or concealment of provider branding. Treat remote provider upload as an external disclosure subject to actual consent and provider privacy terms.
