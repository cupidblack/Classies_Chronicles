## 3. Immutable narrative-anchor classification

Before introducing new context, identify the narrative's **3–7 central hard anchors** when feasible: protagonist/agency, decisive action, particular conflict, causal hinge, essential relationship, earned result and unresolved sequel obligation. Classify each anchor:

| Label | Meaning | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `HARD` | Causally indispensable to the prior story and/or locked accepted canon | Preserve its recognized identity and required ordering; an editor must approve a material change. |
| `SOFT` | Important scene, motif or relationship that can be reinterpreted | Reframe through new evidence if the cause/effect chain survives. |
| `OPTIONAL` | Decoration, exposition, minor dialogue or redundant scene | Remove, relocate or replace when it improves coherent integration. |

**Story-lock and evidence-lock are separate.** A verifiable source fact remains tied to its provenance, but fictional action inspired by it need not assert the source caused an actual event. A character's belief may be mistaken, whereas narrator-level presentation must not assert unsupported real-world events. Preserve the original story's recognizable identity even when its meaning shifts. Do not repeatedly overwrite a scene merely to mention every input domain.

## 4. The exact master §19 eight-operation loop

At **every** V1–V6 transition, perform exactly these eight operations, in this order:

1. **READ** — inspect the actual source body and evidence, date, audience, permissions and fact/fiction classification; record failed reads.
2. **DECOMPOSE** — extract actors, events, chronology, motifs, tension, conflicting claims, risks, choices and meaningful implications.
3. **LOCK** — state current `HARD`/`SOFT`/`OPTIONAL` anchors and independent evidence facts; record how the new context may modify meaning without losing identity.
4. **CONNECT** — choose an actual causal, thematic, relational, physical, historical, visual, environmental, temporal or logistical bridge. If the bridge is forced, reject the source for public use.
5. **TRANSFORM** — rewrite the predecessor's scenes/dialogue/transitions so at least one choice, obstacle, consequence, stake or interpretation changes. Reconcile the whole draft after local edits.
6. **PRESERVE** — verify hard anchors, essential prior events, accepted canon, character knowledge/location and causality still survive the rewrite.
7. **VALIDATE** — check provenance, rights, clock geometry, accepted mission and real-world claims, scene continuity, originality and new narrative consequence.
8. **REGISTER** — store source ID, target stage, pre/post-scene references, anchor effect, bridge, changed choice/consequence, accepted/rejected source outcome, unresolved issue and actual test/approval status.

Alternative verbs `RELATE`, `CHOOSE`, `RECONCILE` and `PROVE` in the Compilation-3 GitHub proposal may describe helpful sub-actions but are **not** replacement canonical operation names. An application that rewrites stage V3 from a fresh blank prompt instead of V2 fails cumulative derivation even if the resulting standalone passage is elegant.

## 7. Scene-level validation and cross-context conflict handling

**Positive causal test:** identify at least one *before* and *after* change for each adopted stage, with a scene and character decision or consequence. **Preservation test:** all hard anchors, accepted facts and person-specific knowledge/location survive. **Rights test:** source quotation/adaptation/transfer is authorized for the requested audience, otherwise omit or rewrite independently. **Reality test:** verified historical/current facts are correctly attributed; Probe future remains fictional; fiction does not report real Koware activity. **Prose test:** the finished V6 reads as a single organic story rather than six essays, sermons or news bulletins.

When contexts conflict, preserve both evidence records, name the conflict with severity/owner, use the higher applicable authority for its own domain, and block or safely omit unresolved material. A new verse cannot change the actual accepted chapter calendar; a live Probe response cannot demonstrate a character was physically at HQ; fictional Edubeam travel does not prove scientific time travel; a diary with read permission cannot be used for voice cloning. Do not conflate story-world beliefs with narrator assertions.

## 9. Deterministic acceptance and failure probes

1. A source adds only an unrelated headline paragraph: `REJECTED_FOR_STORY` even if true.
2. V3 drops the V1 hard conflict: `FAIL_PRESERVATION`; restore the consequence/relationship and retest.
3. A verse provider lacks a dated selection: do not reattribute a fallback as that provider.
4. A 250-entry allocation exists but the requested week mapping is not approved: classify as missing allocation; never infer its entry from arithmetic alone.
5. A mission participant remains deployed in accepted state: their sudden HQ appearance is invalid without a new accepted transfer.
6. A speculative Probe headline is presented as confirmed future journalism: `FAIL_PROVENANCE`.
7. An actual project proposal is described as completed: `FAIL_REAL_WORLD_BOUNDARY`.
8. The cited diary exists but adaptation consent is missing: `RESTRICTED`; do not quote or transfer.
9. An appealing alternative ending violates an accepted prior event: keep canon and revise the ending or record a conditional proposed branch.
10. A sixth-stage story is well written but the V2→V6 trace is absent: document the absent evidence and do not claim that transformation QA was run.
11. The public story ends with a private production note: `FAIL_OUTPUT_BOUNDARY`; separate B and rerun Q12/Q16.
12. An explicit six-version derivation commission: produce six **complete** internal narratives with actual references, not six outlines or a single V6.
