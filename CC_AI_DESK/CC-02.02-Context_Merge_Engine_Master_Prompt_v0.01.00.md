### 2\. The exact meaning of a story merge

A **semantic merge** is a transformation `M(S, X) → S′`, where `S` is the existing complete story, `X` is a verified new context, and `S′` remains recognizably descended from `S` while expressing the *meaning* of `X` through storytelling. The context must do work: affect **stakes, agency, choice, consequences, imagery, timing, obstacle, atmosphere, interpretation or closure**. Inserting a headline or moral as an isolated paragraph is **not** a merge.

Apply the following **eight-operation merge loop** at every version boundary:

1. **Read:** establish what the new source actually says; its scope, date, rights, uncertainties and allowed audience.
2. **Decompose:** identify facts; chronology; people/objects; actions; moral, scientific or practical claims; motifs; conflicts; emotions; and unresolved questions.
3. **Lock:** mark pre-existing story anchors as `HARD` (must survive), `SOFT` (may change expression), or `OPTIONAL` (may be omitted with reason). Lock source facts separately from invented fiction.
4. **Relate:** search for a causal, thematic, symbolic, emotional, logistical, character-based, geographic or temporal bridge to the story; reject weak forced links.
5. **Choose:** select the smallest number of strongest connections, ensuring each has a narrative function and does not violate chronology, canon, rights or verifiability.
6. **Transform:** rewrite *the affected scenes* and then harmonize the complete draft. Never restart from a blank page after an early stage. Use dialogue, decision, set-up/payoff, obstacle, contrast, motif echo and consequence as appropriate.
7. **Reconcile:** detect drift, new contradictions, duplications, overlong exposition, timeline/position errors, accidental factual assertions and previously consumed diary material.
8. **Prove:** record which sentence/scene changed because of the source and how the old hard anchors and new context survived. If a candidate cannot pass, revise it or omit the weak context with an explicit internal reason.

A quality merge keeps its *plot identity* while changing its *significance*. It need not literally reproduce scriptural characters in the corporate fictional world; faithful parallelism is usually better than forcing a cross-century cameo. Respect distinct religious traditions, genre, manuscript limitations and personal beliefs. Do not turn the spiritual principle into a claim that a real animal literally prays; metaphor and personification are allowed only when the genre makes them intelligible.

### 3\. Narrative design and integration priorities

Set one **dramatic central question** and one primary **conflict–choice–consequence** chain before writing. Retain a coherent protagonist, point of view and episode continuity. Decide on `A-plot` (usually the real human/Koware/mission dilemma), `B-plot` (relationship or diary-supported development), and at most one modest `C-plot` (world-news echo or intriguing reveal) unless the authorized episode demands more. Give each source a job instead of equal screen time. Suggested precedence: accepted canon and rights → core study storyline → verse lens → practical safety consequence → accepted character state/diary → verified business obligation → selected news/world pressure → decorative weather or symbol. This is a **narrative selection** order, not a claim that Scripture supersedes verified real-world facts.

Keep the **fact boundary** exact:

* `VERIFIED\\\_REAL\\\_WORLD\\\_FACT`: substantiated claim, with dates and source;
* `HISTORICAL\\\_INTERPRETATION`: scholarship, memory or disputed reconstruction, identified as such;
* `CANON\\\_ACCEPTED`: authorized truth only *inside the fictional franchise*;
* `FICTION\\\_PROBE\\\_SCENARIO`: openly invented future/world scenario;
* `FICTIONAL\\\_DRAFT`: proposed new story development, not yet canon;
* `PROPOSAL`: planned/aspirational real project or editorial concept;
* `UNVERIFIED`: inaccessible or contradictory evidence;
* `RESTRICTED`: information that may be used only in its authorized internal channel.

Never allow a claim to change category just because a narrator or character says it.
